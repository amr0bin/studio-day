The year, in two parts

My subject is nature. We don't only live in nature; we are made of it: water, mineral, weather, time. A face caught before it settles is wilderness too — unmapped, alive, unfinished.

These are the two things I run every year. One is how the work gets made and released; the other is how the year gets closed. Both are built to be opened on a studio day and read in a minute, not studied.

Why these two

I have roughly thirty-five hours a month for making and a further nine to seventeen for everything else. Those hours are scarce, so they have to be meaningful rather than administered. Anything that adds a recurring obligation competes with the making, not with rest.

So the year holds two commitments and no more. The studies series is the working rhythm: one size, one price, one premise, an hour a piece, a monthly release, and a portion of every sale to Arts Umbrella. The annual book is the ending: the writing, the process and the work gathered into one volume each November, so that December stays clear.

Everything settled is written down so it isn't decided again. Everything genuinely undecided is listed as open rather than papered over.
