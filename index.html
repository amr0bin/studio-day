The studio day

Sunday, ten until six, at a table that takes up most of a small apartment. Monday evening, two to four hours after the day job, for writing and documenting and everything else. That is the week. Four projects run inside it, and nothing runs outside it.

The hours

Eight hours on Sunday. Two to four on Monday. Roughly thirty-five hours a month of making and between nine and seventeen of everything else, including shipping, correspondence, bookkeeping and editing.

Those are the real numbers, and every decision in these files was made against them. A project that needs a fifth thing does not get added. A project that turns Monday into a second job gets cut back until it fits. I would rather do four things at a depth I can stand behind than six at the surface.

The table is the constraint underneath the hours. It is where I work, and it is also where I eat, so the day has to close with the table clear. That has shaped the format of the work as much as any aesthetic decision.

Monday, after work

One block, two to four hours, holding everything that is not making. It is the bottleneck in the whole system, so it runs on a rotation rather than on whatever feels most urgent.

  • Week one. Photograph the finished pieces, number them, write a line for each, list them.
  • Week two. Video edit, first pass.
  • Week three. Video edit, finish and publish.
  • Week four. Orders, packing, post office, bookkeeping, correspondence.

The weekly essay is drafted and published on its own schedule, separate from this rotation. It is writing rather than admin, and it does not compete with shipping for the same hour.

Video editing is the item most likely to overrun. If a month gives me only two Mondays, the video is what waits.

The shape of the year

Studies run from January. Course preparation begins in spring and the module is recorded across the summer, when the studies drop to one Sunday a month. The writing runs the whole way through. In September the year's material starts being gathered, and the annual book is finished in November.

December is a retreat. It holds my birthday and the anniversary of a death in my family. Nothing launches, nothing ships, and nothing needs me. The November deadline exists so that stays true.
