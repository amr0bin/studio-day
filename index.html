Sunday, ten until six

  1. 10:00
    Set up already done. Kettle, paper out, palette laid. Twenty minutes of gesture from life or from the file to loosen the hand.
  2. 10:30
    Studies, one an hour. Three before lunch. Number each sheet on the back as it comes off the table.
  3. 13:30
    Lunch away from the table.
  4. 14:15
    Studies, or the month's Studio II assignment, by which Sunday of the month it is. Three more hours.
  5. 17:15
    Stop while it still wants to go on. Log the sheets: number, medium, subject. Note one sentence for Monday's writing.
  6. 17:45
    Rinse, cover, leave the palette. Photograph nothing today.

Tuesday to Friday, twenty minutes

  1. a.m.
    Before anything else: hands, feet, heads, from life or the mirror. A cheap sketchbook, never the good paper. Nothing kept, nothing photographed, nothing posted. This is what rebuilds the hand; the Sundays only keep it.

Monday evening

  1. 1
    Record shot of every sheet from Sunday, on the copy stand, same settings.
  2. 2
    Catalogue entry per sheet. Same fields every time. Nothing is decided about showing it.
  3. 3
    Every second Monday, the Patreon piece, from the fortnight's sentences. Weekly again once the log shows three months of Sundays kept.
  4. 4
    One named person written to. A collector or a reader; not an institution until the rhythm has held. Once a month, the sheets to the critic.
  5. 5
    Check the Calendar for anything inside thirty days.

Capacity holds two of making, video and selling admin. Not three. This year: making, and being seen. Foundations waits for 2028. The site takes five minutes on Sunday morning and is then closed.

For the days it gets heavy: four hours, a sitter, an easel and a timer, and a room of strangers painting the same face on television, succeeding in self-knowledge and courage as much as in likeness. What if this is just for fun?

    Why this stands

    The shape of the week

    Eight hours on Sunday, ten until six, for making. Two to four hours on Monday evening for documentation, correspondence and admin. That is about thirty-five hours a month of studio time and between nine and seventeen for everything else. Those two blocks are the whole budget, and several plans were cut because they did not fit them.

    The governing principle

    One project per area for at least a year. Depth requires boundaries. Anything not currently running is deferred rather than cancelled, and each deferred thing is added only when the one before it has become easy.

    What funds the time

    The binding constraint is paid hours rather than audience or talent. A grant returns hours to the practice; a sale consumes them. Ranked by hours returned: grants, institutional commissioning, teaching priced to institutions, then retail. The Calendar holds the funders and prizes that follow from that ranking.

    Where study sits

    Further study in fine art is a hope I hold open and prepare for. If I am able to study fine art more in my lifetime it will be a great privilege, and it is not an option at present. My near-term path runs through disability studies and counselling psychology, funded by my career. Teaching, writing and studying in fine art are live futures; the Writing page is built so that if the door opens, I am ready.
