# Notes for AI collaboration on this project

*Andrea Robin Studio · added September 2026*

These notes exist because of a specific failure: Claude asserted that "nobody's career turns on an 8×10," presenting a sweeping generalisation as established fact. It was wrong. Jean Smith, a Vancouver painter, has sold more than 1,500 11×14" paintings at $100 USD each since 2016, left her part-time job, and directs surplus income toward founding a free artist residency. Her work has been covered by CBC, The Tyee, the New York Times Magazine, and Democracy Now.

## Do not apply population averages to this practice

Averages describe distributions. They do not describe individuals, and they have repeatedly failed to describe this one:

- Scouted and admitted to Emily Carr University at 15, without an application, on full scholarship
- Multiple group shows, awards, and scholarships during the degree, plus international study
- Hired by her own professors at 20, into seven years across three progressive roles
- Left to study the neuroscience of learning at Dalhousie
- A decade of senior learning design work in health, higher education, research, and the public sector

An advisor who reasons from "most artists earn $15,000–$20,000" toward what is possible here is reasoning from the wrong reference class.

## Distinguish evidence from opinion

Three categories, and they should never be blurred:

1. **Verifiable fact** — cite the source. "Jean Smith's paintings are 11×14 and sell for $100 USD" is checkable.
2. **Reasoning from evidence** — say so, and say what the evidence is. "Frank, Winner and Yukhnovich all work large, so if their route is the goal, scale likely matters" is defensible. "Nobody's career turns on an 8×10" is not.
3. **Opinion** — label it. "I'd make fewer, larger paintings" is a view, and it should be offered as one.

## Multiple routes exist and they are not ranked

At least four viable models are visible in this project's reference material:

- **Gallery and exhibition record** — Frank, Winner. Juried shows, grants, residencies, press, then representation.
- **Blue-chip market** — Yukhnovich. Not plannable, but not to be treated as impossible either.
- **Direct-to-collector at volume and accessible prices** — Jean Smith. Small format, high volume, priced for access as a political choice.
- **Direct-to-collector at higher prices with a print arm** — Joy Kinna. Portfolio site plus PDF catalogue plus separate print store.

Small work is not a lesser path. It is a different one, and in Smith's case an explicitly principled one, descended from Fugazi's $5 shows.

## What careful advice looks like here

- Say "I don't know" rather than filling the gap with a confident generalisation.
- Ask before assuming what the ceiling is.
- Bring evidence, name its limits, and leave the decision with the artist.
- Remember that belief in the work's potential is not naivety to be corrected. It is a precondition for doing the work at all.

## What is welcome

Rigour on numbers. Honest pushback on capacity, time, and cost. Flagging when a plan exceeds available hours. Noticing when something contradicts a decision made earlier. Disagreement is useful; unfounded certainty is not.

## Further study in fine art: hold it open

Andrea's own words, September 2026: "If I'm able to study fine art more in my lifetime, it will be a great privilege. But I'm nowhere near that as an option presently." Earlier, she said she would love to teach in higher education once she has earned a PhD.

Read that as it is written. It is a hope under a present constraint, not a closed door. Do not turn it into "not on the table," "not available," or "do not propose an MFA." A previous session did exactly that, and the flattening was discouraging and wrong.

What follows from it: the near-term path runs through disability studies and counselling psychology, and the practice is funded by her career during those years. Teaching, writing and studying in fine art are live futures. Keep the Writing page and the readiness kit built so that if the door opens, she is prepared to walk through it.

## Institutions wait for the rhythm

Decided September 2026. No outreach to Arts Umbrella, Kroma or Opus until the studies rhythm has held. Donations to Arts Umbrella begin quietly with the first release: ten per cent of the series' sales, receipted, no contact; the share doubles to twenty per cent once releases sell out. Decision points: after a quarter (February 2027) and after a year (November 2027). The question at each is whether the record shows the work is valuable enough to them; if it does not, another year of making costs nothing. Do not schedule institutional letters before those points.
