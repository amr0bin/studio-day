# Foundations and the annual book

*Andrea Robin Studio · September 2026*

Two commitments that sit alongside the studies series. Both draw on the same year's work rather than adding separate production projects.

---

## The annual book

**Not up for debate.** Each year the writing, the documentation of process, and the artworks are compiled into a book. It closes the chapter.

**Completed in November**, every year.

December is wintering. It holds a birthday and the anniversary of a significant family loss, and it is kept as a retreat — no commitments to anyone but herself wherever that is possible. The November deadline exists so that December is genuinely clear.

**What this means practically:** no launches, no live sessions, no drops requiring fulfilment in December. Anything already recorded can keep selling on its own; nothing should need her. This runs against the commercial grain — December is a strong month for art sales — and the decision stands anyway.

**Why the book is cheap to make.** The writing already exists. The documentation happens in the Monday block regardless. Compilation is an assembly task, not a new body of work. It belongs in Monday blocks through October and November, not in Sunday studio time.

## Foundations

### The premise

Everything covered in Foundation year at Emily Carr, reconsidered through fifteen years of professional work in learning. The question it answers: what would I want to learn now, if I could recreate my time at Emily Carr as an adult with experience?

### Why it's strong

Frank and Winner teach subject-specific courses — colour, the self-portrait, composition — aimed at painters who already know what they're missing. Foundations addresses people with no framework at all: adults returning to art, self-taught painters who never had formal training, people who did a degree decades ago and want to rebuild. A larger and less served audience, and a highly motivated one, because what they lack is structural rather than technical.

The premise is also hard to copy. Anyone can teach drawing. Almost nobody can say: I went through this curriculum, then spent fifteen years professionally studying how people learn, and here is what I'd change.

**A third layer, optional but distinctive.** Foundation year is often taught in ways that damage people — comparison, critique culture, the body as a problem to be corrected. The thesis is about precisely this. Foundations could reconsider not only what is taught but the conditions under which it becomes learnable. That would make it unmistakably hers rather than a better version of something that already exists.

### Structure

**Foundations is the multi-year series. Each annual course is one module.** Drawing, colour, composition, the body, materials — whatever the natural divisions turn out to be.

This solves the "what's this year's course" question permanently, builds a coherent body rather than scattered one-offs, and gives someone who takes the first a reason to return for the next four. It's the thing Frank and Winner have that isolated courses don't.

### Format

**Six weeks, three hours per week** — the same rhythm as Foundation year at Emily Carr. The form is part of the argument.

Eighteen hours is substantial. Frank's courses are four sessions of roughly two and a half hours, so ten. Nearly double supports a higher price and makes the annual module a real course rather than a workshop.

**Taught live, recorded as a byproduct.** This is the decision that makes 18 hours affordable. Pre-recording 18 hours means filming, re-filming and editing — plausibly 40 hours. Teaching live means showing up and teaching; the recording exists afterward. Frank's model: take it live or watch the recording the same day, with past courses sold afterward at a lower price. Six weeks of evenings, once, produces an asset that sells for years.

### The arithmetic

| | |
|---|---|
| Delivery | 18 hours (6 × 3) |
| Prep | 12–18 hours (2–3 hours per session) |
| **Total** | **30–36 hours over six weeks** |

Roughly one month of total available time, concentrated into a six-week block. It has to displace something — most sensibly the studies series pauses for those weeks, or drops to one Sunday.

### Pricing

- Live cohort: **$350–$500**, defensible against Frank's $185–$350 for ten hours
- Recording afterward: **$185–$250**
- Twenty students at $400 is $8,000 for six weeks of evenings, and the recording keeps earning

Compare to the studies series at $200 a piece. The difference in return per hour is the reason this branch matters.

### First run

**Small and cheap.** Ten or twelve people, largely from the Emily Carr network, at a reduced first-run price. It produces the recording, the testimonials, and real evidence of what works before charging full price. It is also a much easier ask of that network than anything else on the list.

### Cautions

- Keep Emily Carr out of marketing language in any way implying affiliation or endorsement. Her own experience is hers to teach; seven years on staff means she'll know how that reads from the inside.
- "Foundations" is generic — hard to search for, hard to protect. It may work better as the internal architecture than the public title.

## The annual calendar

| Period | Focus |
|---|---|
| **January–spring** | Studies series running. Painting. Course preparation. |
| **Summer** | Foundations module taught live. Studies series pauses or halves. Must sit outside term time once the MSc begins. |
| **Autumn** | Studies series resumes. Recording goes on sale. Book compilation begins. |
| **November** | Book completed. |
| **December** | Wintering. No commitments. Nothing that requires her. |

Summer delivery gives spring to prepare and autumn to sell the recording, and keeps the six-week block clear of coursework.

---

## The leverage

One year of work, four outputs: the paintings, the studies series, the course, and the book. The writing is already done. The documentation happens anyway. The course footage comes from sessions being taught live rather than filmed separately.

The book is a compilation task. The course is a sequencing task. Neither is a new production project.
