The rules behind the numbers

Cotton paper is my base. I add the surface premium consistently across every size:

Cotton paperbase price
Unprimed linen+ $20–$30
Cradled panel (exhibition)+ $50–$70

My panel work sits at the top of each size band because it carries the most material and finishing.

Prints — edition of 10. Every copy in an edition holds one price. When I release a new edition, I look back at the previous one: if at least 6 of 10 sold, I read that as my pricing working and my practice moving, and I set the new edition 10–15% higher. If fewer than 6 sold, I hold the price and consider whether the work, the format, or something else needs to shift first.

Originals — by size and surface. I track sales quietly by type (for example, all 8×10″ linen pieces). Once a type is consistently finding homes — roughly after 4–6 have sold — I raise my starting price for the next piece of that type by 10–15%.

Two things I hold to: I never change the price on a piece that's already listed, and I never communicate these reviews to buyers as pressure. This is internal bookkeeping that keeps my pricing honest to my practice.

Direct (my website / Shopify): I use the prices as listed.

Gallery or shop (40–50% commission): I gross up so my net stays protected, then keep my direct price publicly consistent with the gallery's — or I negotiate exclusivity.

My total per print is ~$2.50–$4.50, which I round to a flat $5 added to my print floor across all sizes. A backing board goes with every print to keep it flat in transit and to signal care when the package is opened.

GST — federal (5%). I only register and collect once my worldwide revenue passes $30,000 CAD in a single quarter, or across four consecutive quarters. Below that I'm a "small supplier" and registration is optional. Once I cross it, I register with the CRA within 29 days. Registering also lets me claim GST back on business expenses. GST does not apply to US and international sales, as those are exports.

BC PST (7%). Original artwork I create and sell is PST exempt in BC. Limited edition prints are generally exempt too as reproductions of my original work — I'll confirm this with the BC Ministry of Finance or a tax professional before treating prints either way.

Deductions I file on a T2125: my home studio share (⅓ of rent, utilities, internet, tenant's insurance); art materials and supplies; finishing and shipping; equipment and software; professional development; marketing; and business use of vehicle. I keep every receipt, and I work with an accountant familiar with Canadian artist taxation — there are real nuances between a business and a hobby under CRA rules.

A note for whoever is helping me with this. This is my working reference, and I'm grateful for the help keeping it consistent. Two things matter most: I never quote a price below the floor shown for a piece, and I never change the price on a piece that's already listed. When something isn't covered here, please hold off and check with me rather than estimating.

All figures in CAD, using midpoint estimates: ~$375 materials and ~$500+ studio overhead per month, ~$62/hr labour, across ~6 pieces/month. My markup runs 30–50% above floor.

Four tiers: limited edition prints (ed. of 10) · original paintings and drawings · studies (irregular batch drops) · card packs (printed reproductions).

Price reviews happen privately and apply to new work only. I refine my floors as I track my real hours and materials.
