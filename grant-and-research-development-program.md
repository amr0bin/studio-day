# Grant and research development program
*Andrea Robin Studio · September 2026*

A system for knowing where financial support for the practice comes from, how to get it, and being ready when the practice is running and making is habitual. Adapted from the four-week onboarding used as Grant and Research Development Officer at Science World, scaled to one artist, one project, and the hours that exist.

No amounts and no deadlines are recorded here. Both change every year. What is recorded is what each thing is, why it fits, and what has to be verified before a plan is built on it.

---

## 1. Why this program, and the rules it runs under

Decisions already made in this project that this program has to respect:

- **Funded time beats retail income.** Ranked by hours returned: grants, institutional commissioning, teaching priced to institutions, then retail. A grant returns hours to the practice; a sale consumes them. This program serves the top of that ranking.
- **Capacity.** About 35 hours a month for making, 9–17 for everything else, and the Monday block is already committed to documentation. Grant work has to fit inside the 9–17, alongside documentation, and mostly *out of* the same material documentation produces.
- **One project per area for at least a year.** The grant program applies with one project. It does not chase every open call.
- **Apply municipally and provincially before federally.** A first grant on the record changes how every later application reads.
- **Reason from this practice, not from averages.** Published success rates are useful for deciding how many applications to make. They are not a ceiling on what this practice can do (see `advising-notes.md`).

## 2. What translates from the Science World model, and what doesn't

| Science World (week) | Organizational version | Studio version |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Audit the existing program and five years of results | There is no program yet and the grant record is zero. The audit is a **readiness** audit: what exists, what's missing, what a funder would recognize as a project. |
| 2 | Calendar, case statements, templates, master file, prospect cultivation | Same, smaller. The case statement is a project description in three lengths. Cultivation means one conversation with a program officer before applying. |
| 3 | Standard answers, funder expectations, the admin-percentage question | Same. The studio equivalent of the admin question is **artist fees** — pay yourself in the budget at the CARFAC rate. |
| 4 | Goals, renewals vs. prospecting, risk, reporting, systems | Everything is prospecting for now. The risk question becomes *which first application is most likely to succeed*. Reporting still matters: it is what makes the second grant easier than the first. |

**Grant Connect / Imagine Canada** is the wrong database for this. It indexes foundation funders who mostly give to registered charities. The equivalent for an individual artist is free and small: the BC Alliance for Arts + Culture funding page, plus the program pages of BC Arts Council and Canada Council read directly. The fifty candidates in `grant_sources_canadawide_and_in_bc_for_creatives_1.pdf` are the discovery work already done; what remains is filtering.

## 3. The system

Six parts. Each is a file or a habit, and the whole thing lives in the studio repo alongside everything else.

### 3.1 Prospect pipeline
One spreadsheet or markdown table, `grants-pipeline.md`. One row per opportunity:

funder · program · stream · what it funds · eligibility notes · typical intake (verify each year) · fit (strong / possible / no) · status (researching / preparing / submitted / result) · date verified · source link · notes

The "date verified" column is the discipline. A row older than a year is stale and gets re-checked before it is trusted. Sources to check when refreshing: the funder's own site first; BC Alliance for Arts + Culture funding page; CARFAC BC; Canada Council and BC Arts Council newsletters (subscribe once).

### 3.2 Master file
`grant-master-file/` — everything reusable, so no application starts from a blank page:

- **Project description** at 100, 250, and 500 words. One project, written once, trimmed to each funder's field.
- **Artist statement** — the version that says what the thesis inquiry says, in plain language, for a peer jury that hasn't read the curriculum.
- **Bio** at 50 and 150 words. **CV** in Canada Council / BC Arts Council format (exhibitions, awards, education, publications, teaching, residencies).
- **Image sheet** — ten works, titled, dated, dimensions, medium, sized to each funder's spec. This is what the Monday documentation block produces; the two programs share an output.
- **Budget template** — expenses and revenue, cash and in-kind shown separately, artist fees as a line, with a full-project version and a grant-specific version.
- **Work plan template** — months down the side, activities across.
- **Standard answers** (see 3.4).
- **Support material list** — who can write a reference, what letters of support exist, where the writing samples are.

### 3.3 Calendar
One year, one page: intake windows for the strong-fit rows in the pipeline, working back six weeks for a first application and three for a repeat. Overlaid on the studio calendar so grant weeks don't land on the same Sundays as a series release. Living document; refreshed when the pipeline is.

### 3.4 Standard answers
Funders ask the same five things in different words. Draft each once, well, and keep them current:

1. What will you make, and what is it for? (the project, concretely)
2. Why now, and why you? (the practice, the record, the inquiry)
3. What changes for your practice if this is funded? (impact — the answer is *hours*, said in the funder's language)
4. How will the money be spent? (budget narrative, fees, timeline)
5. How will you know it worked, and who will see it? (outcomes, dissemination, exhibition or publication plan)

Add funder-specific standard answers as they recur: equity and access statements, land acknowledgement where asked, community engagement, and any AI-use disclosure a funder requires (several councils now ask — verify each funder's current policy).

### 3.5 Application process
A checklist run for every submission:

1. Re-verify eligibility, deadline, and requirements against the funder's *current* page.
2. Contact the program officer with one or two specific questions. Note what they say in the pipeline.
3. Assemble from the master file. Tailor the project description and answers to the assessment criteria, in their order, using their words.
4. Budget balances; fees in; cash and in-kind separated.
5. One outside reader — someone who does not know the project — reads the narrative and the budget together and says what the project is. If they can't, revise.
6. Submit at least two days early. Portals fail on deadline day.
7. Log it: date, program, amount requested, materials used.

### 3.6 Results, reporting, and records
- `grants-log.md`: every application, the result, the feedback, and what was changed as a consequence. This is the "five years of results" for the future version of this program.
- Ask for jury comments on every result, funded or not. Both councils provide them on request.
- Funded projects get a folder: agreement, payment schedule, receipts, interim and final reports, images of the outcome. Final reports are written from the log, not from memory.
- Bookkeeping: grants are taxable income in Canada, reported on a T4A, with rules for deducting the expenses of earning them. See `tax-and-deductions-reference.html` and keep receipts from day one of a funded project.

## 4. The funder map

Tiered by the order of application, not by size. Each entry: what it is, why it fits, and what to verify. Amounts and deadlines belong in the pipeline, dated, not here.

### Tier 1 — municipal and regional (apply first)
- **The municipality's cultural grants.** The Atlas lists the City of Vancouver, but municipal programs are tied to the city boundary. Verify which municipality's program the studio is actually eligible for, and whether it funds individuals or only organizations. If it is organizations only, the route is partnership — a community organization, gallery, or arts council as applicant, with the studio as the artist.
- **Metro Vancouver Regional Cultural Project Grants.** Regional, project-based, modest. Verify current eligibility for individuals.
- **Vancouver Foundation.** Regional rather than discipline-specific. Fits a practice that sits between art, disability studies and counselling psychology. Priorities shift; read the current ones before assuming fit.
- **Local arts council or gallery calls** — juried exhibitions and small honoraria. Not grants, but the exhibition record they build is what makes Tier 2 read well.

### Tier 2 — provincial
- **BC Arts Council — Individual Arts Grants (Visual Artists).** The natural first serious application. Provincial residency met; a professional practice with an exhibition and teaching record is the profile it is built for. Verify current streams, the definition of "professional artist," and whether there is an early-career stream that fits better.
- **BC Arts Council — Professional Development.** Smaller, faster, and fits mentorship, a residency, or focused technical study. Often the easiest first win.
- **BC Arts Council — Individual Arts Grants (Creative Writers).** Dear Ordinary is a body of nonfiction. It may be eligible in its own right, as a separate project, once the visual project has its grant. One project per area — but writing is a different area.

### Tier 3 — federal (apply once there is a record)
- **Canada Council for the Arts — Explore and Create** (Research and Creation is the component most relevant to developing a body of work). The largest source of funded studio hours available to a Canadian artist. Requires a validated applicant profile, which takes time to set up and be approved — do this in the readiness phase, not the week before a deadline.
- **Canada Council — Professional Development for Artists.** Same profile, smaller ask.
- **Canada Council — literary streams** for the writing, on the same logic as the BC Creative Writers note.

### Adjacent — not arts grants, but funded time
- **Graduate scholarships.** The education path runs MSc → MA → PhD. SSHRC Canada Graduate Scholarships, the BC Graduate Scholarship, and institutional awards fund study time, and study time is time not spent earning elsewhere. The master file's CV and statement are most of an application. Verify eligibility against enrolment status each year.
- **Prizes with cash awards** — Kingston Prize, Salt Spring National Art Prize, and the international portrait competitions in the Atlas. Entry fees are expenses; wins are income and record.
- **Institutional commissioning and residencies** — second on the hours-returned ranking. Tracked in the Atlas rather than here, but the same master file serves them.
- **Nomination-only awards** (Hnatyshyn, Sobey). Cannot be applied for. Known so that the record is built with them in view.

## 5. Readiness — what has to be true before the first application

- [ ] One project defined, with a name, a scope, a timeline, and an end state a jury can picture.
- [ ] CV current and in council format.
- [ ] Artist statement written for a jury, not for the thesis.
- [ ] Ten documented works at funder spec (the documentation project delivers this).
- [ ] Canada Council applicant profile created and validated; BC Arts Council portal account set up.
- [ ] Budget template built, with CARFAC fee schedule to hand.
- [ ] Two people willing to be references or write support letters, asked in advance.
- [ ] Pipeline populated and dated; calendar drawn for the next twelve months.
- [ ] Bookkeeping in place to receive and account for grant income.

## 6. The four-week setup, at studio scale

Four sessions of two to four hours, drawn from the non-making hours. Not four full weeks.

**Session 1 — Inventory and pipeline.** Run the readiness checklist. Populate the pipeline from the fifty-source list and the Atlas, filtering to strong and possible fits. Verify Tier 1 and Tier 2 intake windows against the funders' sites and date each row. Set up the council profiles (they take days to validate; start now).

**Session 2 — Master file.** Write the project description at three lengths. Bring the CV and bio into council format. Draft the budget template with fees. Pull the image sheet from the documentation archive.

**Session 3 — Standard answers.** Draft the five answers. Read the current assessment criteria for the first two target programs and check every answer against them.

**Session 4 — Plan and targets.** Draw the calendar. Choose the first two applications. Define success for year one as *submitted on time with a reusable master file*, not funded. Decide the go/no-go rule for anything else that appears mid-year (below). Set the refresh rhythm.

---

## 7. The rhythm after setup

- **Monthly**, inside the non-making hours: check the calendar, move anything due within six weeks into preparation. About an hour.
- **Per application**: six to ten hours for a first application to a funder, three to five for a repeat. Two applications a year is realistic inside current capacity alongside documentation. Three is the ceiling.
- **Quarterly**: refresh the pipeline — re-verify rows, add anything new from the funder newsletters, drop what no longer fits.
- **Annually**: review the log. What was submitted, what was funded, what the jury said, what changed. Re-run the readiness checklist. Redraw the calendar. This is the Week 1 audit the Science World model assumed, and after the first year it exists.

**Go/no-go for an unplanned opportunity:** apply only if the project is the one already defined, the master file covers at least three-quarters of the application, and the deadline does not fall in a release week. Otherwise it goes in the pipeline for next year.

## 8. Additional considerations

**The definition of "professional artist."** Both councils define it, usually as specialized training, peer recognition, a public presentation history, and a commitment to practice. Read the current definition and make sure the CV demonstrates each element. The Emily Carr foundation year, the group shows, the awards, and the teaching are the evidence; they need to be visible.

**Public money has terms.** Public funders have explicit priorities and reporting conditions. Accepting the grant means delivering the project as described, on the timeline, and reporting on it. Scope the project to fit the hours before writing the budget, not after.

**The Monday block is shared.** Documentation and grant readiness overlap almost entirely — the image sheet, the CV, the statement are the same work. Treat grant preparation as an output of documentation, not a fourth project.

**Program officers are the cultivation strategy.** One call before each application. Ask what a strong application in this stream looks like this year and whether the project fits. They will say.

**Peer assessment.** Both councils recruit peer assessors, and serving on a jury is the fastest education in what juries respond to. Once there is a record, volunteer.

**Rejection is data.** Ask for comments, log them, change one thing, reapply. Most first applications to a funder are not funded; most artists with a grant record got it on a second or third try. This is a statement about the process, not about this practice's ceiling.

**AI use.** Some funders now ask whether AI was used in preparing an application, and some restrict it. Verify the current policy of each funder before submitting, and keep the writing the artist's own. The master file is a set of drafts the artist writes and owns.

**Writing and painting are two eligibilities.** Dear Ordinary as nonfiction and the paintings as visual art can each be a project, in different streams, in different years. Don't apply for both at once. Do know that both doors exist.

## 9. Decisions still open

1. Which one project the first year's applications are built around: the studies series, the Dear Ordinary paintings, or a defined body of work for a juried show.
2. Which municipality's program applies, and whether it funds individuals.
3. Who the two references are.
4. Whether the first application is Professional Development (smaller, faster, higher odds) or a project grant (more hours, bigger return).
5. When to begin — the readiness checklist says when, not the calendar.
