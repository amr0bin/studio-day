My responsibilities — what running this practice actually requires

☰
What I commit to doing to make this work
Organised by area · realistic and honest about the time involved
  • ✦
    Make the work Two studio Sundays a month on studies, six an hour apart, so twelve are ready for each release. The other two Sundays are held for larger work, which is made now and goes on sale in January 2028. The work itself is the foundation of everything else. No marketing substitutes for a strong, consistent body of work.
  • ◷
    Track my time and costs accurately I keep a simple log of actual hours per piece and actual materials costs each month. This is how I refine my floor costs and make sure my pricing stays honest. Without this, my numbers are guesses.
  • ◻
    Photograph and catalogue every study before it is listed Good photography of my work is non-negotiable. On the first Monday of the month I photograph each study, including one capture at print resolution, give it its number, and write a short honest description. A sold study cannot be photographed again, and the April 2027 print review depends on these files.
  • ↗
    Release on the fixed days My list sees each release on the first Saturday after the 15th. It opens to everyone on the Monday night that follows. First come, first served, no holds. I note when each study sells, because the fastest are the candidates for prints.
  • ✉
    Pack and ship orders carefully, in one session Every study ships in a glassine envelope with an acid-free backing board, in a rigid mailer, with a handwritten card. I pack the whole release on the Monday after it goes public. This is how people become repeat buyers: from a good experience receiving my work.
  • ◈
    Write to my list once a release One plain letter a month through Kit: the studies, their prices, and a sentence on what I was looking at. No sales language. No countdown timers. This is how I stay in touch with the people who collect the work.
  • ▶
    Post YouTube process videos when I have something genuine to share I don't post on a schedule for its own sake. When I have a process, an idea, or a story worth sharing, I film and edit it. YouTube's search-based discovery means a good video keeps finding people long after I post it. Quality over frequency.
  • ☰
    Keep clean financial records every month I record all income and expenses monthly, keep all receipts (digital or photographed), download my Shopify statements, and file everything in a folder organised by year and month. At tax time I hand this to my accountant — it should require no heroic effort because I've stayed on top of it throughout the year.
  • ◈
    File my taxes correctly and on time I file a T2125 with my personal T1 return each year, claiming all legitimate deductions. I pay any balance owing by April 30. I monitor my revenue against the $30,000 GST threshold and register proactively when approaching it. I work with a Canadian accountant familiar with artist taxation.
  • ↑
    Review my price after every release When a release sells out, the next one is priced 15–20% higher, rounded to a clean figure. When it does not sell through, I hold the price. I never lower it, and a listed study keeps its price until it sells. This is a private, internal review. I never communicate it to buyers.

How I optimise financially — without compromising my integrity

My approach to financial optimisation is simple: make the most of what I'm already doing, price my work honestly, reduce unnecessary costs, and keep more of what I earn through legitimate tax deductions. I don't use pressure tactics, artificial scarcity, countdown timers, or manipulative language of any kind — ever.
◈
Pricing and income optimisation
Things I do to earn fairly from my work — all grounded in real value
  • 1
    Keep the studies as the one entry point Studies are the least expensive way into my work and, for the first year, the only work on sale. One size, one price per release, one rhythm. Nothing is sold below them, so nothing I make competes with them.
  • 2
    Raise the price only when a release sells out A sold-out release earns the next one 15–20% more. In this scenario that takes a study from $200 to $265 in three releases, and it is the single biggest financial lever I have. The increases reflect my growing practice and track record. They are honest and earned.
  • 3
    Build my list consistently Email converts better than any social platform. Every person who buys from me or signs up from my website joins the list, and the list sees each release a weekend before anyone else. I grow it steadily and treat it with care.
  • 4
    Pass shipping costs to the buyer I charge actual shipping at checkout rather than absorbing it into my prices or offering free shipping. This protects my margins, especially on international orders where Canada Post rates are significant ($45+). Buyers understand this — it is standard practice for independent artists.
  • 5
    Give a share, and let it grow with the work Ten per cent of each release's sales goes to Arts Umbrella as an ordinary receipted donation, doubling to twenty once a release sells out. It is counted in this scenario as a cost, so the profit shown is what remains after giving.
  • 6
    Hold prints until the studies have chosen them Prints are reviewed after the April 2027 release. The studies that sold fastest become limited edition archival prints, 8×10" only, at $125, offered as pre-orders that close. Print on demand is considered once prints pass $3,000 a month. Card packs are looked at in the same review.
  • 7
    Hold larger work until January 2028 The larger pieces I make on the other two Sundays are not sold yet. When they open, they open to people who already own a study, at prices rebuilt from tracked hours and from the study price at that date.
  • 8
    Gross up prices for galleries and shops If a gallery or shop takes 40–50% commission, I calculate my list price so that my net after commission equals what I would earn selling direct. I never give a gallery my direct price — that would mean earning half what my work is worth. My gallery list price = my target net ÷ (1 − commission rate).

Tax and expense optimisation
Keeping more of what I earn through legitimate deductions — see my full tax reference document
Claim my home studio
I deduct 1/3 of my rent, utilities, and internet as a business expense — this is the single largest annual deduction available to me. At $1,500+ rent, that's $500+/month or $6,000+/year off my taxable income.
Deduct all materials
Every paint, canvas, panel, paper, ink, and brush is a deductible business expense. I keep every receipt. At $375/month in materials, that's $4,500/year in deductions.
Deduct platform fees
Shopify fees ($253 in this scenario alone), Format subscription, Kit email platform, and domain costs are all deductible. I save all invoices automatically.
Buy supplies before December 31
If I need materials, equipment, or supplies for my practice, buying before year-end means I deduct them in the current tax year rather than waiting until next year.
Register for GST at the right time
Once registered, I claim back GST on all business expenses (Input Tax Credits). On $375/month in materials alone, that's roughly $225/year back from the CRA — plus GST on all other deductible expenses.
Deduct my accountant
The cost of my accountant is itself a deductible business expense. Good tax advice pays for itself — the deductions it protects far outweigh the fee.

Realistic expectations — what this scenario assumes and what might differ

◈
What to expect vs. this best-case model
Honest context for a first launch
  • ~
    Three sell-outs in a row is the ceiling, not the forecast If a release does not sell through, the price holds at $200, the Arts Umbrella share stays at 10%, and the unsold studies stay listed at their price. At 60% sold over the three months (22 of 36), revenue is $4,400. After fees, giving, materials and studio overhead, that pays me about $57 an hour for the 36 hours of making, and 14 studies remain listed, worth $2,800 at their price.
  • ~
    A study may take longer than a weekend to find its person I don't worry if a study sits for a few months. I don't discount it or chase the sale. Sold studies stay visible, and an unsold one keeps its price until it sells.
  • ~
    My audience needs time to build 193 visitors/month at launch with no active shop is a seed, not a crowd. The list takes time to develop, and the direct, individual invitations matter most at the start. Later releases will reach more people than the first — this is normal and expected. Consistency over time is the strategy.
  • ~
    Floor costs may be higher or lower than estimated The ~$111 floor is an estimate: one hour a study, and overhead shared across twelve studies a month. Once I track actual hours and actual materials spend for one full series, I'll have real numbers to work with. If my floor is higher, I adjust prices accordingly — I never sell below floor.
