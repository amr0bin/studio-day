# Commission in care — proposal pack

*Template, 18 September 2026. For Studio II assignment 12, October 2027, and for the commission that sits at the centre of the 2029 to 2031 movement on the Ceiling page. Written once so the proposal is assembly rather than production.*

**The rules that do not move:** proposed in writing, with a budget, at CARFAC rates. As a commission or a paid artist-in-residence period. Never entered as a contest. Never free. Made *with* an organisation rather than *for* one.

---

## Before writing anything

1. **Find out who commissions.** Vancouver health authorities and their foundations, hospices, mental health and disability services. Which of them run arts programmes, who runs them, and whether they have ever paid an artist. **Unverified**, and the single most useful thing to find out.
2. **Go once as a visitor.** A proposal from someone who has never been in the building reads like one.
3. **Read Hospital Rooms.** The British model already on the Atlas, for how the case is made.
4. **Have the consent documents ready.** A proposal that arrives with its consent and image-use process attached is a different kind of document from one that does not, and it is the part that distinguishes an artist from a supplier.

---

## The proposal, in seven parts

### 1 · What is proposed
Two sentences. A body of drawings made with people in ______, over ______ weeks, resulting in ______.

### 2 · Why this artist
The BFA, the years of studio teaching, the practice on the body and care, and the disability studies work. State the relevant facts with dates and stop. No adjectives.

### 3 · What the people involved get
First, and in their terms rather than mine: a record print for everyone who sits, a public talk in their own room with the people who sat, and work that stays if they want it. The community is the first audience.

### 4 · What the organisation gets
Named artworks for their walls, images they may use in their annual report with consent, and a documented programme they can point to. Be specific about what they may and may not use.

### 5 · How consent and access work
The sitter agreement and the image-use release, attached. Who can decline and how. How withdrawal works. Access in the showing: image descriptions, hanging height, plain-language versions. Disability justice as a condition of the work rather than an accommodation.

### 6 · The plan
Weeks, sessions, who is present, what happens if someone is unwell, and what happens if the work does not go well. A named contact on their side.

### 7 · The budget
See below. Always attached. Never a number in a sentence.

## After delivery

| Step | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| The public talk, in their room | The part most artists skip, and the part that makes it engagement rather than production |
| Every work catalogued, with its consent status | The corpus ledger, and the condition of any later deposit |
| The fee recorded in `submissions.md` | Evidence of a professional practice, which several funders define partly by paid work |
| A written account of what the project found | The scholarly ledger. Placed with a journal, or deposited. See `orcid-and-zenodo.md` |
| The organisation thanked in writing, and kept | The relational ledger. A partner who would do it again is worth more than the commission |

---

## Why this one project carries so much

It deposits into four of the five ledgers at once, and it evidences research ethics literacy, knowledge mobilization and community engagement, which appear as three separate criteria on most major fellowship forms. Very few doctoral applicants in a fine arts department arrive with funded work delivered inside a health setting, under consent, with a fee paid and a talk given.

Which is the argument for doing it properly and slowly, and for never doing it for free.
