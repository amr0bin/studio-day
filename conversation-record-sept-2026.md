# Conversation record — strategy and platform decisions

*Andrea Robin Studio · September 2026*

What was worked through, what was decided, and what was ruled out. Kept so the reasoning doesn't have to be rebuilt from scratch.

## The postcard club: closed

**Dear Ordinary** was designed as a monthly snail mail club — a small envelope, a letter wrapping a postcard-sized print, a hand-drawn tea cup sticker. $15/month domestic, $17 international, or $150 and $170 annually.

The economics don't work. Floor cost per postcard is roughly $20–25 before postage, so each member costs $6–11 a month. More members means more loss and more fulfilment work.

Platforms were researched thoroughly:

- **StampFans** — prints and mails on your behalf, keeps ~$3/subscriber/month. Ruled out: it holds the subscriber list, and it would replace a handmade envelope with a printed one posted from the US.
- **MailClubly** — free with an 8% fee, or $9/month at 0%. Canada Post rates built in, prepay plans, Stripe payouts, CSV export. The best fit *if* the club were happening.
- **Posthouse** — free under $100/month then 4.5%. Native domestic/international tier pricing and a prelaunch waitlist, but no annual billing. (Not Australian, despite the .se domain.)

**Decision: not running the club.** Not because of admin — automation would have removed the friction but not the loss. Because it consumes hours and returns none, and the hours are the scarce thing.

Dear Ordinary as a body of work continues. It was never really a subscription; it's a 56-week thesis inquiry.

## The high-ticket question

Ali Abdaal's $100K-in-a-year model rests on digital products with near-zero marginal cost, sold to an audience built over years. His principle holds — make once, sell many, protect your energy. His sequencing doesn't transfer: in this field credibility precedes teaching income rather than following audience growth.

The instinct behind it is still right. Fewer, higher-value transactions mean less admin, because packing a $600 original takes the same twenty minutes as packing a $46 card pack.

## What the reference artists actually show

- **Caitlin Winner** — painted on weekends while working in tech. Visible arc roughly 2021–2025: Manifest biennial, Royal Institute of Oil Painters, First Street Gallery, the Martha Boschen Porter Fund, a Vermont Studio Center residency, prizes, a cookbook commission, a Studio Visit cover, Hyperallergic, then Carrie Haddad and Galerie Mokum. Teaches online courses and runs coaching circles.
- **Zoey Frank** — gallery representation, grants and international prizes. Roughly one new online course a year at $185–$350: live, recorded same day, then sold as evergreen recordings. Teaching is the income layer on top of reputation.
- **Flora Yukhnovich** — MA in 2017, found on Instagram by a dealer, seven-figure auction sales within four years, now Hauser & Wirth with Victoria Miro. Not a plannable route, and not to be treated as impossible either. What transfers is a singular visual language with real art-historical grounding.
- **Jean Smith** — Vancouver. 11×14" portraits at $100 USD on Facebook since 2016, 1,500+ sold, quit the day job, raised over $200,000 toward a free artist residency. Monthly expenses of $1,000; everything above went to the fund. Typical month around 30 paintings; peaks of 80 and 93. Sold within minutes of posting, in named series, to repeat collectors. Preconditions: 35 years of cultural standing through Mecca Normal, and a New York Times Magazine feature in 2021. At peak she worked 15-hour days, seven days a week.

**The correction that matters:** none of the first three built careers through e-commerce funnels. Their route was juried shows, prizes, grants, residencies, press, then galleries — with teaching as the income that follows credibility. Smith took a genuinely different route to a real outcome. Small work is not a lesser path.

## Platform decisions

| Tool | Role |
|---|---|
| **Format** | Portfolio and front door. Landing page is the paintings; click through to series with process and exhibition images (the Caitlin Winner structure). |
| **Originals** | PDF catalogue plus reply-by-email purchase, invoice, payment plans on request (the Joy Kinna model). No cart, no listings, no inventory sync. |
| **Shopify** | Prints and studies only, if and when needed. Joy Kinna runs a separate print store linked from her portfolio site. |
| **Kit** | The mailing list. The only asset that survives any platform closing. |
| **YouTube** | Discovery. Search-based, evergreen, no schedule. |
| **Patreon** | The writing. Login-gated, so not in the open crawl. |

**One body of work, several surfaces.** A single painting can become an original, a print, a process video, a week of writing, and a course module. The leverage is more surfaces from the same act of making, not more products.

## The writing

Patreon stays. The writing is part of the work, not marketing for it.

Two different meanings of "safe" were separated:

- **Safe from being taken** — Patreon's login gate handles this about as well as anything can. Nothing makes writing uncopyable. Canadian copyright exists automatically from the moment of writing.
- **Safe from being lost** — Patreon does nothing here, and this is the likelier risk.

**The arrangement:** the markdown repo is the archive of record. Git provides dated version history, valuable for a thesis and as proof of authorship. Publish *from* the archive; never write in the platform. Keep at least one copy outside GitHub. Consider Zenodo for selected essays if a citable, permanent record matters later.

**YouTube was considered as a writing home and rejected.** Google confirmed to CNBC that it uses YouTube videos to train Gemini and Veo 3, and told a federal court in June 2026 that its terms grant broad training rights. Uploading grants a worldwide, sublicensable, transferable licence. The third-party training setting, off by default, doesn't cover Google's own models. It would be worse than Substack on exactly the grounds that prompted the move. It's also not a writing surface, and video production is the heaviest admin in the plan.

**What works instead:** one essay a month becomes a narrated process video. The essay is the source, the video is the doorway.

## Capacity — the arithmetic

Eight hours on Sundays, two to four on Monday evenings. About 35 hours a month for making, 9–17 for everything else.

Six pieces a month across three sizes is roughly 36 hours of work. That is the ceiling, not the rhythm — no slack for a failed piece or a missed Sunday.

The Monday block is the real bottleneck. Photography, listing, packing, shipping, bookkeeping, catalogue updates and video editing do not fit. Video editing alone is 4–8 hours.

**Capacity exists for two of: making, video, and selling admin. Not three.**

---

## The year, as agreed

Protect the Sunday block and the writing. Spend the Monday block on documentation rather than video. Sell originals by PDF and email. Build no business.

Deferred, not cancelled: the video, the course, the prints store, the studio sales, the club. Each gets added only when the thing before it is genuinely easy.

The principle is Andrea's own: one project per area for at least a year, because depth requires boundaries.

## Funded time beats retail income

The binding constraint is paid hours, not audience or talent. A $25,000 grant and $25,000 of print sales are not equivalent — the grant returns hours, the sales consume them.

Ranked by hours returned: grants, institutional commissioning, teaching priced to institutions rather than hobbyists, then retail.

**Further study in fine art, in Andrea's words:** "If I'm able to study fine art more in my lifetime, it will be a great privilege. But I'm nowhere near that as an option presently." It is a hope held open, not a door closed. The near-term education path runs MSc Disability Studies → MA Counselling Psychology (toward RCC), with a PhD after; teaching in higher education is the aim. During those years the practice is funded by a professional career and runs alongside part-time graduate study. An earlier version of this note flattened the sentence above into "not available" and "do not propose an MFA"; that was an inference, not her position, and it is withdrawn.

**The coherence is real, though.** Disability studies, counselling psychology, and a thesis on the body as the site of the sacred are one inquiry approached from three directions. Week one argues you cannot sense another person's inner life if you were trained to ignore your own — a counselling claim, a disability studies claim, and a painting practice at once.

## Where this landed

The **studies series** — see `studies-series-plan.md`.

Jean Smith's model read correctly: a dedicated series with one format and one rhythm, constraints as creative fuel, funding a social good. The same conditions are present here — a table in a small apartment, an hour per piece at a level of skill where the work is immersive rather than frustrating, and a genuine connection to Arts Umbrella through a grandmother who taught her to paint at three.

## A note on how this went

Mid-conversation, Claude asserted that "nobody's career turns on an 8×10." Jean Smith's are 11×14. The claim was false, and the reasoning — generalising from three artists to a rule about art careers — was worse than the error.

See `advising-notes.md`. The short version: population averages have repeatedly failed to describe this practice, and projected limitations are not useful here. Arithmetic about a specific plan on a specific calendar is welcome. Unfounded certainty about what is possible is not.
