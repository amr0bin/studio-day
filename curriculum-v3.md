# The Curriculum, version 3

A five-year practice on the shape of a graduate program, 2026 to 2031. Written at the resolution I can know: the five years on one page, each term on one page, the first year month by month, the week once. Years two to five are rewritten each November from the portfolio, not from this plan.

Voice: my own. Sources: only titles I can hold. Names: none that are private. Laurels: none.

---

## Part I. Architecture

### What this is for

Three life goals share one practice: accessibility in learning, including experiential learning; counselling psychology for disability and mental health, possibly leading toward art therapy; and study and teaching in fine art. The studio is where the three meet. Drawing and painting the body from life, with the body understood as the site of the sacred, is the inquiry that a doctorate in contemporary arts could one day hold, that a care organisation could one day commission, and that a classroom could one day be taught from. This curriculum builds that practice back from inconsistency and carries it far enough that any of the three doors can be walked through when it opens.

### The three requirements

Every assignment, module and destination in this document meets all three, or it is cut.

1. It deepens my knowledge and skill in drawing and painting, so that my perspective can be expressed more fully.
2. It prepares the portfolio for meaningful grants, scholarships, awards and opportunities, including ones I do not yet know exist.
3. It puts me in the company of people who see and understand the largest opportunities in art without greed or ego, inside the bigger context of what art means in society and history.

Skill, portfolio, belonging.

### The capacity rule

Sunday ten to six for making. Twenty minutes on weekday mornings. Monday evening for the record, the writing and one letter. Capacity holds two of making, video and selling admin, never three. This year: making, and being seen. Foundations begins in 2028. The Patreon piece is fortnightly until the log shows three months of Sundays kept. The site and this document take five minutes on a Sunday morning and are then closed.

### The nine courses

Every graduate program in visual art is nine things underneath the degree. They run every year; their weight changes.

| Course | What it is here |
|---|---|
| Studio I | The studies, every Sunday, and the twenty weekday minutes. Volume, numbered and logged. |
| Studio II | Twelve assignments a year on the two larger-work Sundays. Each has a constraint, a part that is mine, and a destination. |
| Technique | My own Foundations, taken as its first student: sixteen modules in four stages, one a month, October 2026 to March 2028. |
| Critique | The Federation's Peer Critique evenings on Granville Island, and one critic whose eye I trust, monthly. |
| Seminar | One text a month, read with a pencil; one talk a month; two hundred words in the journal after each. |
| Exhibition | Proposal-based, in real rooms: consignment, juried opens, members' walls, and later a room of my own. |
| Visiting artists | One painter a quarter, approached through their work in writing for Dear Ordinary. The interview is the introduction. |
| Field school | One week a year away with paintings; from year two, one week inside a painter's workshop. |
| Thesis | Dear Ordinary, fifty-six pieces; the annual book each November as its defence; the year-five exhibition as the viva. |

Two lines the first draft added, kept at monthly resolution: **Money**, what this month costs, earns and applies for; and **Care**, what the interior needs this month for the work to continue.

### Evaluation, designed first

The practice is measured by five things and nothing else, looked at on the first Monday of every month and rebuilt at each quarter.

1. The studies log: sheets made, numbered, released, sold, kept.
2. The sitting log: who sat, when, where, from life.
3. The critic's sentence: one true thing said about the month's work, recorded.
4. The portfolio: twelve images rebuilt each quarter against the six questions every panel asks. Can I draw from life at speed and sustain a drawing to resolution. Do I get a likeness. Can I handle a light problem and a colour problem. Is there a series. Is there a large piece. Is there a work where the concept is visible without being explained.
5. The room: the names of people who have seen the work this quarter, and where.

Each term has one measure drawn from these five. Feeling on a Sunday evening is not one of them.

### The decision points

Two dates decide the shape of the next year, from the record rather than the plan.

- **February 2027**, a quarter in. Has the rhythm held: Sundays kept, sheets released, the critic seen monthly? If yes, the giving relationship with Arts Umbrella may be approached; if not, wait for November.
- **November 2027**, a year in. The portfolio and the critic's view decide: Foundations in 2028 or not; two studio Sundays a month or four; Patreon weekly or fortnightly; and whether the work is valuable enough to institutions to write the first letters, Arts Umbrella with the giving record attached, the paint maker with a year of paintings.

Each November after that, the coming term is rewritten from the same evidence.

### Standing rules

No private names anywhere in the practice's documents. No recitation of achievements; there is only the next brief. Granville Island is the direction: when there is a choice of rooms, the Island's come first, because time there is happiness, proximity to the studio I mean to have, and neighbours. Institutions wait for the rhythm. Capture is separate from showing. The concept enters the making, not only the writing. And joy is a rule: when the practice gets too serious, four hours, a sitter, an easel and a timer, and the memory of a room of strangers on television painting the same face, succeeding in self-knowledge and courage as much as in likeness. What if this is just for fun? It is.

## Part II. The five terms

### Term one, 2026 to 2027. The hand back, and being seen

**Aim.** Rebuild the hand and the eye from inconsistency to reliability, and be seen by people who can see: a critic, a room of peers, two juries, one consignment committee.

**Studio.** The studies every Sunday from October; the twenty minutes every weekday. Twelve assignments, one a month, December clear. Technique modules 01 to 12 in sequence.

**Critique.** The Federation's Peer Critique on 25 September, then monthly. The critic found there or nearby by the end of September and seen every month with the month's sheets.

**Seminar.** Twelve texts (Part V) and twelve talks: SFU's 611 Talks, the Contemporary Art Gallery, the Vancouver Art Gallery.

**Exhibition.** Art Rental & Sales in April. The Royal Society of Portrait Painters' open in February if the self-portrait holds. The Kingston Prize in September. Salt Spring in May. The members' wall at Basic Inquiry in August.

**Visiting artists.** Four painters approached in writing, one a quarter; the first interview published by spring.

**Field school.** The Audain and the Island, a weekend with the Carr room and the coast.

**Thesis.** Dear Ordinary fortnightly; the book in November.

**Money.** Donations to Arts Umbrella from the first release, ten per cent of sales, quietly. BC Arts Council registration in October and the exhibition-fee record checked, which decides whether the provincial grant is open. Canada Council profile validated. Application fees under $200 for the year.

**Care.** The shyness worked as a problem, not a fact: capture separate from showing, one public wall, one crit a month. December kept clear.

**Measure.** The twelve-image portfolio answers the six questions, and the critic's twelve sentences are on file.

### Term two, 2027 to 2028. One painter's week, one cohort

**Aim.** Study inside one living painter's practice for a week, and gain a cohort: the room from a workshop, the cohort from a residency.

**Studio.** Modules 13 to 16 through March 2028, then the sixteen written up as the pages Foundations will teach from. Studio II becomes eight larger assignments, four on panel.

**Critique.** The Island evenings continue. The critic monthly. From March, a second critic if the first is right about what I cannot see.

**Seminar.** Practice-based research read as method, not shelf: Sullivan, Barrett and Bolt, Loveless, Chapman and Sawchuk, so that the studies can be framed as research-creation in any application.

**Exhibition.** Kingston and Salt Spring again, from a stronger portfolio. The Federation's opens. Art Rental & Sales, resubmitted or continued.

**Visiting artists.** Four more interviews. One of them is the painter whose workshop I attend.

**Field school.** A week inside one painter's workshop, chosen from the Atlas by whose decisions I most need to understand. MacDowell applied for in February 2027 for a fortnight in autumn 2027.

**Thesis.** Dear Ordinary weekly if the log allows. The second book.

**Money.** BC Arts Council Visual Artists intake if eligible. The first Foundations stage recorded in July and on sale by the end of August, priced low to the first ten.

**Care.** The MSc coursework begins in this term; the capacity rule holds it to two lines. Nothing is added.

**Measure.** A sitting from life every month, and the critic says the likeness holds.

### Term three, 2028 to 2029. A body of work, and a room

**Aim.** Twenty pieces with one thesis, on paper, panel and linen, and a room to hang them in.

**Studio.** Studio II becomes the body of work. Technique becomes teaching: Stage 2 recorded.

**Critique.** A crit group of three or four painters, met through the Island and the interviews, meeting monthly with work on the wall.

**Seminar.** Art history of the figure, the face and the table, read as a syllabus: Fried, Alpers, West, Brilliant, Bryson, Clark.

**Exhibition.** A two-person or solo show proposed in writing to the public and artist-run rooms on the Atlas that accept proposals. The commission in care delivered and shown in the organisation's room.

**Visiting artists.** The interviews collected as a section of the third book.

**Field school.** London or The Hague: the portrait galleries and the drawing rooms, a week.

**Thesis.** One piece of writing placed with a journal on the Writing page.

**Money.** The care commission paid at CARFAC rates. A Canada Council Explore and Create application for the body of work, framed as research-creation.

**Care.** Counselling psychology coursework alongside; the practice is the place the two disciplines meet, not compete.

**Measure.** Someone who did not know me writes about the work.

### Term four, 2029 to 2030. Placement, and the larger decision

**Aim.** Consistent placement, and the study decision made from evidence.

**Studio.** Larger work at pace; Stage 3 recorded.

**Critique.** The crit group continues; a curator invited to one session.

**Seminar.** The doctoral literature: Elkins, Borgdorff, Nelson, and the SFU PhD handbook read as a document.

**Exhibition.** A consignment program, a gallery relationship that arrived organically, a public collection on paper.

**Visiting artists.** A visiting-artist talk given, not attended: the Federation, a community centre, or a classroom.

**Field school.** A Banff thematic residency if one fits the body; otherwise Madrid or Bologna.

**Thesis.** The prospectus written as if for admission, whether or not it is submitted.

**Money.** A larger grant applied for on the record of three years.

**Care.** The MA in Counselling Psychology reaches its practicum; the studio holds at Sundays only if it must.

**The decision.** SFU's PhD in Contemporary Arts is a research-intensive, practice-based doctorate of four to five years, open to applicants holding an MA or MFA with high standing, or an established professional artistic practice; domestic tuition; applications open each October for the following fall. By this term the practice will have five years of sittings, four books, twelve interviews, a room of my own work and a commission in care, which is what "established professional practice" means on paper, and the MA will be in hand or near. The decision is made from the portfolio, the critic's view and the three life goals, and it can be yes.

**Measure.** Work sells before it is finished.

### Term five, 2030 to 2031. The exhibition that reads as a direction

**Aim.** The thesis defended in a room.

**Studio.** The exhibition body. Stage 4 recorded; Foundations complete.

**Critique.** The crit group reviews the hang.

**Seminar.** The catalogue essay written, by me or by the writer from term three.

**Exhibition.** The show a curator can write about: the body as the site of the sacred across five years, with the sittings, the writing and the care commission inside it.

**Visiting artists.** The cohort is now people I have sat with, drawn, written about and shown beside.

**Field school.** Wherever the doctorate, if begun, sends me.

**Thesis.** The fifth book is the catalogue.

**Money.** The practice funded by its record: grants, teaching, sales, in that order of hours returned.

**Care.** Whatever the three goals now ask.

**Measure.** The best I have been, by the portfolio and by the room.

## Part III. Year one, month by month

Each brief holds the assignment, the module, the crit, the seminar, the destination, the money and the care for the month, and one line on how the concept enters the making. The week is in Part IV and is not repeated.

### September 2026. Before the rest

The rhythm restarts: paper torn, palette laid, numbering from S001. Weekday minutes begin. Register for the Federation's Peer Critique on the 25th and bring one piece, the sheet I am least sure about; stay the whole evening and critique others. Look for the critic in that room. Decide the Vermont Studio Center question by the 30th and let MacDowell's September round pass in favour of February. Maggee Day's *Passing Notes* at Equinox from the 12th: the studies' method on a gallery wall; go twice. Money: nothing applied for; the exhibition-fee record checked. Care: the first evening on the Island is the first step toward the studio there.

### October 2026. A song, in sixty gestures

**Assignment 01.** Pencil, 8 × 10, two minutes each, from life or the mirror, never a screen; sixty across the month, six kept. The song is mine and stays unnamed. Destination: the six open the Patreon series. **Module 01, visual literacy**, with 02 underneath: how to look at and talk about a drawing before making one. **Crit:** the six to the critic; the Island evening if one runs. **Seminar:** Berger, *Ways of Seeing*; one 611 Talk. **Destinations and money:** BC Arts Council registration done; Canada Council profile started; City of Vancouver CASC noted as a sponsored route only. Skowhegan's call closes the 16th; apply only if nine weeks of leave is conceivable. **Care:** nothing is kept, so nothing can fail. **The concept enters:** the body moving before it is judged is the first sacrament of the practice.

### November 2026. One sitting, resolved

**Assignment 02.** Pencil, half sheet, one person who agrees to sit for three hours in one afternoon, a word from them before the first line; no retouching after. Destination: the sitter's record print; the sitting log; the first Kingston-qualifying drawing. **Module 02, line and mark-making.** **Crit:** the drawing to the critic; the Island evening. **Seminar:** Brilliant, *Portraiture*; the Culture Crawl as the month's talk, 19 to 22, as a visitor. **Destinations and money:** the first release on the first Saturday if stock allows; ten per cent to Arts Umbrella, quietly; the annual book compiled on Mondays and finished by month end. **Care:** asking someone to sit is the first act of the shyness being worked; the answer will be yes more often than feared. **The concept enters:** the sitter's word is the body's claim on the drawing.

### December 2026. Clear

No assignment, no module, no release. One year-end letter to the list. The tax question to the accountant with the year-end books. The studies log and the sitting log read once, honestly, for the February decision. Weekday minutes continue if they want to; the point is that nothing needs me.

### January 2027. Self, in five values

**Assignment 03.** Acrylic on paper, one lamp, five values mixed before starting, the mirror not a photograph; the answer to a question I have been avoiding. Destination: the Royal Society of Portrait Painters' open, closing early February, if it holds. **Module 03, value and light.** **Crit:** the critic sees the first quarter whole. **Seminar:** West, *Portraiture*; one talk. **Destinations and money:** Banff's summer deadline, expected mid-January, only if a project is ready; MacDowell's round opens the 15th. **Care:** the hardest sitter; kindness in the light is a fail. **The concept enters:** a face lit honestly is a body telling the truth about its limits.

### February 2027. The bright ground, shown

**Assignment 04.** Acrylic on paper, the method left visible: bright underpainting at the edges, muted layer, gloss; whatever is on the table at six. Destination: a process post; the Art Rental & Sales package begun. **Module 04, perspective.** **Crit:** the decision point, a quarter in, read from the logs with the critic present. **Seminar:** Bryson, *Looking at the Overlooked*; one talk. **Destinations and money:** MacDowell closes the 10th, applied for with a year of studies behind it; the Portrait Painters' entry submitted or not, decided by the piece. **Care:** the decision is made from the record, not from how February feels. **The concept enters:** the ordinary table, unarranged, is where the sacred is looked for.

### March 2027. A table of things, one light

**Assignment 05.** Cradled panel, 12 × 16, one light source, three surfaces, D-rings and wire before it is called finished; a still life that is a portrait of someone absent. Destination: Art Rental & Sales, closing 30 April. **Module 05, anatomy**, on the weekday minutes: the skull, the hand, the joints. **Crit:** the panel to the critic before the hardware goes on. **Seminar:** Alpers, *The Art of Describing*; one talk. **Destinations and money:** BC Arts Council results expected late March; Vermont's second window closes the 31st. **Care:** finish is a form of respect for the person who will hang it. **The concept enters:** what a body leaves on a table when it is not there.

### April 2027. The sitting, for a jury

**Assignment 06.** A portrait of a Canadian person met in person, after a sitting, in pencil or acrylic, within the Kingston size limit, made inside the window; the sitter brings one object and I decide whether it appears. Destination: the Kingston Prize, entries open mid-month, deadline 1 September. **Module 06, gesture drawing**, under the portrait as its foundation. **Crit:** the sitting drawn twice, the critic chooses. **Seminar:** Fried, *Absorption and Theatricality*; one talk. **Destinations and money:** Art Rental & Sales submitted by the 30th with the package from March. **Care:** a jury is a room; the auditorium is in Toronto in November if the work goes that far. **The concept enters:** what I would want to be seen holding.

### May 2027. Five weeks, one subject

**Assignment 07.** Five 8 × 10s, one a Sunday, same subject, same place, same hour, same medium; released as a set. Destination: the May release; one of the five to Salt Spring by the 31st. **Module 07, figurative**: anatomy and gesture together. **Crit:** weeks four and five to the critic, because that is where the assignment lives. **Seminar:** Clark, *The Sight of Death*, which is this assignment in prose; one talk. **Destinations and money:** Salt Spring's $35 entry; nothing else. **Care:** boredom by week three is the plan working. **The concept enters:** what is left to see when the interest has worn off is what devotion is.

### June 2027. The figure in a room

**Assignment 08.** Cradled panel, 16 × 20 or larger, a person in a real room in window light, three sessions; a room where the body is safe, painted as what makes it safe. Destination: the centre of the second book; the piece the November decision is judged on. **Module 08, portrait**, the most demanding figure subject, taken in the month the figure is at its largest. **Crit:** the Island evening with the panel. **Seminar:** Wollheim, *Painting as an Art*; one talk. **Destinations and money:** the Drawing Prize in early June only if a specific drawing justifies the freight; otherwise nothing. **Care:** the room may be my own; safety is a subject. **The concept enters:** where the body stops being watched.

### July 2027. A master study

**Assignment 09.** Pencil, 8 × 10, from a reproduction, ninety minutes: one painting from the Atlas, its three decisions written on the back. Destination: a Patreon post. **Module 09, colour theory**, read and mixed on the weekday minutes; introduced only now, once value holds. **Crit:** the three decisions discussed, not the copy. **Seminar:** Elkins, *What Painting Is*; the field school as the month's talk. **Field school:** the Audain's Carr room, the Island, a weekend. **Destinations and money:** none; a lighter month by design. **Care:** the summer's looking is the rest. **The concept enters:** what they decided before they picked up the brush is the tradition the practice belongs to.

### August 2027. In public

**Assignment 10.** Three sessions drawn where people can watch, pencil, 8 × 10: an Island bench, the seawall, the Library's roof; one sheet chosen. Destination: the members' wall at Basic Inquiry, or the Federation's if a members' show fits. **Module 10, still life**, as the low-pressure subject to apply value and colour on the weekday minutes. **Crit:** the chosen sheet, and the account of what changed in the line when someone was looking. **Seminar:** Graw, *The Love of Painting*; one talk. **Destinations and money:** the Patreon question decided on the log: weekly again, or not yet. **Care:** the shyness worked where it lives, in a small room on purpose. **The concept enters:** the body seen making is the body as it is.

### September 2027. The large piece, and someone owns it

**Assignment 11.** Unprimed linen or exhibition panel, at least 36 × 48; total freedom on subject, one condition: it belongs to someone else when finished, named before the first mark. Destination: the named owner, or the November decision point. **Module 11, acrylic painting**, the medium itself, at scale. **Crit:** the naming discussed with the critic before the mark. **Seminar:** Bell, *What Is Painting?*; one talk. **Destinations and money:** Kingston's deadline on the 1st with April's portrait; Vermont's autumn window on the 30th; the Federation's autumn open. **Care:** the year's last month; the twenty minutes have become automatic by now or the plan is rewritten. **The concept enters:** the thesis, whole, at the size a wall needs.

### October 2027. A commission in care

**Assignment 12.** A body of drawings made with a care organisation in Vancouver, sittings consented and logged, proposed in writing with a budget at CARFAC rates; never entered, never free. Destination: their walls, a fee, a talk in their room. **Module 12, composition.** **Crit:** the proposal reviewed for ethics and consent before it is sent. **Seminar:** Loveless, *How to Make Art at the End of the World*, read for the frame the proposal needs; one talk. **Destinations and money:** BC Arts Council registration renewed for the December intake; the proposal's budget as the first fee the practice has asked for. **Care:** the proposal may take longer than the month; the assignment is proposing it properly, and the drawings follow when consent does. **The concept enters:** what the drawing is for once it is not for me.

### November 2027. The decision

The second book completed. The portfolio rebuilt against the six questions. The critic's twelve sentences read together. The decision point on the Year: Foundations in 2028; two Sundays or four; Patreon weekly; the first letters to institutions, or another year of making, which costs nothing. Term two is written from this, not from Part II above.

## Part IV. The week, once

**Tuesday to Friday, morning.** Twenty minutes before anything else: hands, feet, heads, from life or the mirror, in a cheap sketchbook, never the good paper. Nothing kept, nothing photographed, nothing posted. This rebuilds the hand; the Sundays keep it.

**Saturday.** Tear paper if the month's is used. Lay the palette, set the kettle. Twenty minutes, then close everything.

**Sunday, ten to six.** Set-up already done. Twenty minutes of gesture. Studies, one an hour, three before lunch, numbered on the back as they come off the table. Lunch away from the table. From a quarter past two, studies or the month's assignment by which Sunday it is, three more hours. Stop while it still wants to go on; log the sheets; write one sentence for Monday. Rinse, cover, leave the palette. Photograph nothing.

**Monday evening.** Record shot of every sheet on the copy stand, same settings. Catalogue entry per sheet, the same fields, nothing decided about showing. Every second Monday, the Patreon piece. One named person written to, a collector or a reader; once a month, the sheets to the critic. The Calendar checked for anything inside thirty days. Stop.

**The first Monday of the month.** The five measures read: the studies log, the sitting log, the critic's sentence, the portfolio, the room. Ten minutes. Then the month's brief from Part III, and the site closed.

## Part V. Seminar syllabus, year one

One text a month, read with a pencil, from the Writing page's verified shelf. The order runs from seeing to method.

| Month | Text |
|---|---|
| October | John Berger, *Ways of Seeing* (Penguin, 1972) |
| November | Richard Brilliant, *Portraiture* (Reaktion, 1991) |
| January | Shearer West, *Portraiture* (Oxford History of Art, 2004) |
| February | Norman Bryson, *Looking at the Overlooked: Four Essays on Still Life Painting* (Reaktion, 1990) |
| March | Svetlana Alpers, *The Art of Describing* (Chicago, 1983) |
| April | Michael Fried, *Absorption and Theatricality* (California, 1980) |
| May | T.J. Clark, *The Sight of Death: An Experiment in Art Writing* (Yale, 2006) |
| June | Richard Wollheim, *Painting as an Art* (Princeton, 1987) |
| July | James Elkins, *What Painting Is* (Routledge, 1998) |
| August | Isabelle Graw, *The Love of Painting* (Sternberg, 2018) |
| September | Julian Bell, *What Is Painting?* (Thames & Hudson, 1999) |
| October | Natalie Loveless, *How to Make Art at the End of the World: A Manifesto for Research-Creation* (Duke, 2019) |

Alongside, one talk a month: SFU's 611 Talks at 611 Alexander Street, free and public; the Contemporary Art Gallery; the Vancouver Art Gallery; the Federation's education program. Two hundred words in the journal after each, separating what the speaker did from what I felt about it.

Year two's syllabus is the practice-based research shelf: Sullivan; Barrett and Bolt; Nelson; Chapman and Sawchuk; Borgdorff; Elkins's *Artists with PhDs*; the *Journal for Artistic Research*. Year three's is the figure, the face and the table read as history. Both are on the Writing page and are set each November.
