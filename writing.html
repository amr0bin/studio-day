Dear Ordinary, week by week

A fifty-six week curriculum for a thesis inquiry that moves between scholarly writing and studio practice, published weekly on Patreon as a studio journal. The curriculum is drawn from the Path to Good star chart: eight constellations of equal dignity, thirty-five stars, no fixed sequence.

The form

Each post opens with an artist, in the middle of something specific: a work, a gesture, a material choice. The week's question arrives obliquely after that. The framework comes third and stays compressed, because the art is the primary event and the theory is there to name what the artists have already shown. Then a second artist, a gathering, and a studio invitation written peer to peer.

Three artists a week, chosen against the standing commitment to centre the work and lives of historically, persistently or systemically marginalised people. Where a work centres embodied experience that is not my own, the interpretation rests on the artist's verified words or on scholarship from inside that experience. I write as a white settler in Canada of Celtic descent. That location is a fact to write from honestly, and it draws a boundary around what my voice has standing to say.

The craft standard

The aim is writing that could stand in an MFA workshop and eventually in a book, which is a different bar from a clean newsletter. Two habits are worth naming because they persist.

The first is the courage gap. The art-historical material is consistently the bravest writing in a post and the personal material is consistently the most guarded. A workshop reader will ask why it is easier to expose someone else's history than my own present. Closing that gap is the highest-leverage improvement available to me.

The second is that no single rhetorical move may become the default route to contrast, emphasis or transition. A phrase-level ban does not hold; the habit returns in different clothes. Week 15 banned not X, but Y and Week 16 complied to the letter while reproducing the identical see-saw as balanced sentence pairs with the conjunction removed.

Ground prose carries the facts, the sequence, the material and the dates, and it is the majority of any post. Star sentences are scarce, roughly one in eight or fewer. A lyrical sentence arriving after six plain ones is a change in altitude the reader feels; the same sentence after six other lyrical ones is noise.

The diagnostic pass

These are invisible from inside the drafting voice and obvious from a count. All six before a draft is finished.

A first draft is expected to fail this. That is a property of fluent prose rather than a sign the draft is bad, and the pass is mandatory rather than contingent.

Every biographical detail, quotation, date and critical position is checked against a real source before publishing, however well-sourced a draft appears.

Where the writing lives

Patreon holds the published essays. It sits behind a login, which is a real improvement over an open platform, though nothing makes writing uncopyable. What Patreon does not do is protect against loss, so a copy of every essay is kept outside it.

Substack was the previous home. I left because the work was open to crawling. YouTube is not an alternative for the same reason, at a larger scale, and it is not a writing surface in any case.

The annual book

Each year the writing, the process documentation and the works are gathered into one volume. The book is the act of closing the chapter rather than a product built to sell.

Nothing in it is made for it. The essays are already written, the process stills come from the monthly videos, and the works are photographed as they are finished. Gathering begins in September, layout runs through October, and the book is finished in November so that December stays clear.

Trim size, print method, whether an edition is offered for sale, and whether the writing appears whole or selected are all still open. Fixing the format once means the volumes read as a set on a shelf and I stop deciding it annually.
