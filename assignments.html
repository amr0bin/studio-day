CurriculumFive years, on the shape of an MFA

Small in a big pond, by design

The full document, at the resolution that can be known, is curriculum-v3.md, rewritten each November. A graduate program in visual art is nine things underneath the degree: open studio production, technique, faculty-led group critique, art history and critical seminars, proposal-based exhibition in a real gallery, free public talks by visiting artists, an international artist in residence who works with students, a field school abroad, and a thesis with a defence. SFU's School for the Contemporary Arts, a twenty-minute walk from here, is built from exactly those, and most of them are open to anyone. This curriculum borrows the shape and runs it for five years alongside a career, so that the practice belongs with the students and educators of the most serious rooms without waiting for admission to one.

What every assignment must do

Three requirements, and an assignment that meets fewer than all three is cut. It deepens knowledge and skill in drawing and painting so that my perspective can be expressed more fully. It prepares the portfolio for meaningful grants, scholarships, awards and opportunities, including ones I don't yet know exist. And it puts me in the company of people who see and understand the largest opportunities in art without greed or ego, inside the bigger context of what art means in society and history. Skill, portfolio, belonging. Each assignment below says how it meets them.

The nine courses, as they run this year

Studio I
The studies, every Sunday, and the twenty weekday minutesOpen studio production: the volume of work that everything else stands on. Logged and numbered.
Studio II
The twelve assignments, one a month, on the larger-work SundaysThe portfolio course. Each has a constraint, a part that is mine, and a destination.
Technique
My own Foundations, taken as its first student: sixteen modules in four stages, one a month, October 2026 to March 2028The drill under the assignments. Each module is worked through on the weekday minutes and one Sunday morning, then written up as the module's page for the course that will teach it. I stay one stage ahead of anyone I teach, and nothing goes on sale that has not been tested on this practice first.
Critique
The Federation's Peer Critique evenings at the Flex Space on Granville Island, and the critic, monthlyFaculty-led group critique: one artwork each, an evening of discussion, a senior member and juror in the room, by donation. The first is 25 September 2026. The Island is also where the practice means to live, so every evening there is a step toward the studio and the neighbours.
Seminar
One text a month from the Writing page, read with a pencil, and one 611 Talk or gallery talk a monthArt history and critical seminar. SFU's 611 Talks are free and public, about four a term, at 611 Alexander Street: visiting artists and curators presenting their practices. The Contemporary Art Gallery and the Vancouver Art Gallery run talks too. Attend, take notes, and write two hundred words for the researcher's journal.
Exhibition
Proposal-based, in real roomsArt Rental & Sales in April, the Federation's opens, the members' wall at Basic Inquiry. A proposal written for each, the way the Perel Committee asks SFU students to write theirs.
Visiting artists
One painter interviewed a quarter for Dear OrdinaryThe artist-in-residence course, inverted: instead of waiting for a visiting artist to come to me, I go to their work in writing, and the interview is the introduction.
Field school
A week away with paintings, once a yearThis year, the Audain and the Island. Next year, a week with one painter's workshop. After that, London, Madrid, The Hague or Bologna from the Atlas.
Thesis
Dear Ordinary, and the annual book each NovemberFifty-six pieces on the body as the site of the sacred is the written thesis; the book is its annual defence. In year five the exhibition is the viva.

Studio II, this year

Twelve assignments from October to October with December kept clear, each made on the two Sundays held for larger work while Studio I runs underneath. Before any of them, one with no destination: the person who will look.

00 · September 2026, before the rest

The seat beside the work

The constraint

One painter whose eye you trust. Not a friend who likes you; someone who draws and will say what is true. Found by the end of September. Shown the month's sheets every month, in person or by post, for the whole year.

Yours to interpret

Who. The Federation's Peer Critique evening on 25 September is the room to look in first: one artwork each, a senior member leading, by donation, on Granville Island. The critic may be someone met there, someone from the Emily Carr years, or a stranger whose work you respect, written to plainly. The evening itself counts as the first crit.

Where it goes

Nowhere. This is the one assignment with no destination, because it is the condition for the other twelve. A log entry is not a crit. Nobody was ever made ready by a mirror.

What it shows

That the work is being seen by someone who can see it.

What fails it

Reaching October without a name. Choosing someone kind instead of someone honest.

Belonging first: the critic is the group critique with one member.

Who will tell me the truth about a drawing?

01 · October 2026

A song, in sixty gestures

The constraint

Pencil only. 8 × 10 sheets torn from the Collection paper. Two minutes each, from life or from the mirror; never from a photograph. Sixty across the month, six kept.

Yours to interpret

Choose one song. Play it on repeat for every session. The gestures are the song's shape in a body, and only you know which song it is.

Where it goes

The six kept sheets open the first Patreon post of the series and go in the file under S numbers.

What it shows

Speed; the hand ahead of the judgement; that the practice has restarted.

What fails it

Any gesture over five minutes. Any sheet from a screen. Fewer than sixty.

Technique: Module 06, gesture; 02, line and mark-making. Skill: the hand. Portfolio: the drawing-from-life images every panel asks for. Belonging: the first sheets shown to the critic.

What does my hand still know that my eye hasn't caught up to?

02 · November 2026

One sitting, resolved

The constraint

Pencil. A half sheet, 11 × 15, torn. One person who agrees to sit, in person, for three hours in one afternoon. No retouching after the sitting ends.

Yours to interpret

Ask the sitter for a word before you begin. Draw them as that word. Do not tell anyone which word.

Where it goes

The sitter receives the record print. The sitting goes in the sitting log with date and place: this is the first drawing that qualifies for the Kingston Prize window.

What it shows

Sustained observation; likeness; that a stranger will trust you with three hours.

What fails it

Working from a photograph. Finishing it later. Not asking anyone.

Technique: Module 08, portrait; 05, anatomy. Skill: sustained observation and likeness. Portfolio: the sitting that qualifies for a portrait prize. Belonging: a stranger trusted you with three hours.

Who would sit for me, and what do I owe them for it?

03 · January 2027

Self, in five values

The constraint

Acrylic on paper, 11 × 15. One lamp, curtains closed. Five values and no more; mix them before you begin and put the tubes away. The mirror, not a photograph.

Yours to interpret

A self-portrait as the answer to a question you have been avoiding. The question stays private; the answer is the painting.

Where it goes

If it holds, the Royal Society of Portrait Painters open, which closes in early February and selects international entries online. If it doesn't, the file, and the next one.

What it shows

Value control; likeness of the hardest sitter; that you will put your own face in front of a jury.

What fails it

A sixth value. Softening the light to be kind. Not entering because it 'isn't ready'.

Technique: Module 03, value and light. Skill: value and self-observation. Portfolio: the self-portrait a jury reads first. Belonging: an open exhibition of a two-hundred-year-old society.

What do I look like when nobody is arranging me?

04 · February 2027

The bright ground, shown

The constraint

Acrylic on paper, 11 × 15. The method left visible: a bright underpainting that stays at the edges, the muted layer over it, gloss to seal. Whatever is on the table at six in the evening, as it is.

Yours to interpret

The table is yours; what is on it is the ordinary. Decide what the bright colour underneath means and let it show through where it should.

Where it goes

A process post on Patreon with the three stages photographed, and the Art Rental & Sales package begun from it, because that jury wants to see a method, not a mood.

What it shows

Colour; that the method is a method; that the ordinary holds a picture.

What fails it

Covering the ground completely. Tidying the table first.

Technique: Module 11, acrylic painting; 09, colour theory. Skill: colour and method. Portfolio: the process images a consignment jury wants. Belonging: the method taught in public on Patreon.

What happens to a colour when it is only allowed to show at the edges?

05 · March 2027

A table of things, one light

The constraint

Cradled panel, 12 × 16. One light source. Objects chosen for their surfaces: something matte, something glazed, something that reflects. D-rings and wire on the back before it is called finished.

Yours to interpret

A still life that is a portrait of someone who isn't in the room. Their objects, or objects that stand in for them. Nobody needs to be told who.

Where it goes

The Art Rental & Sales submission, which closes 30 April. Read the 2026 package now; make the piece the way the package asks.

What it shows

Light on form; composition; finish to a standard a consignment program will hang.

What fails it

Two light sources. A background that competes. No hardware.

Technique: Module 10, still life; 12, composition. Skill: light and finish. Portfolio: the still life submitted to a curated program. Belonging: selection by curators, collectors and designers.

Can a table hold a person?

06 · April 2027

The sitting, for a jury

The constraint

A portrait of a Canadian person you have met in person, made after a sitting, in pencil or acrylic, on paper or panel, within the Kingston Prize's size limit. The sitting logged. Made inside the window that opened 2 September 2026.

Yours to interpret

Ask the sitter to bring one object that matters to them. Whether it appears in the portrait is your decision, not theirs.

Where it goes

The Kingston Prize, entries open mid-April, deadline 1 September: one jpg, a one-page PDF, $40. The auditorium, at last, is in Toronto in November.

What it shows

Everything a portrait prize asks: likeness, life, finish, and the nerve to enter.

What fails it

A sitter you didn't meet. A portrait finished before the window opened. Not entering.

Technique: Module 08, portrait, under conditions. Skill: the portrait under competition conditions. Portfolio: the prize entry. Belonging: thirty finalists in a room in Toronto.

What would I want to be seen holding?

07 · May 2027

Five weeks, one subject

The constraint

Five 8 × 10s, one a Sunday for five Sundays, of the same subject in the same place at the same hour. Either medium, but the same medium all five. Released together.

Yours to interpret

Choose the subject knowing you will be tired of it by week three. What you find in weeks four and five is the point.

Where it goes

The May release, as a set of five, and one of them to the Salt Spring National Art Prize before it closes on 31 May.

What it shows

Seriality; that a subject can be worked rather than visited; discipline across a month.

What fails it

Changing the hour. Skipping a week. Choosing something easy to love.

Technique: Module 12, composition, across a series. Skill: seriality. Portfolio: a series, which every panel asks for. Belonging: a national prize with a BC island at its centre.

What is left to see when the interest has worn off?

08 · June 2027

The figure in a room

The constraint

Cradled panel, 16 × 20 or larger. A person in a real room, from life, in daylight from the window. At least three sessions.

Yours to interpret

A room where the body is safe. It may be your room. Decide what makes it safe and paint that, not the furniture.

Where it goes

The centre spread of the annual book, and the piece the November decision point is judged on.

What it shows

Scale; the figure in space; that the concept can be painted without being explained.

What fails it

A room from a photograph. Explaining it in the title.

Technique: Module 07, figurative; 04, perspective. Skill: scale and the figure in space. Portfolio: the large piece. Belonging: the book that goes to the file libraries.

Where does the body stop being watched?

09 · July 2027

A master study

The constraint

Pencil, 8 × 10, from a reproduction, since Foundations is being recorded this month and the Sundays are halved. One painting from the Atlas: one of the Carrs at the Audain, a Hammershøi, a Morandi, the Fabritius.

Yours to interpret

Copy it to find its decisions, not its surface. Write the three decisions you found on the back.

Where it goes

A Patreon post: the study, and the three decisions. The habit of reading a painting this way is the real product.

What it shows

That you can read a picture; that a small study can hold a large idea.

What fails it

Copying the surface. More than an hour and a half.

Technique: Module 01, visual literacy. Skill: reading a painting's decisions. Portfolio: none, on purpose. Belonging: the history the whole practice sits inside.

What did they decide before they picked up the brush?

10 · August 2027

In public

The constraint

Three sessions drawn where people can watch: a Basic Inquiry session, the seawall at English Bay, the Public Library's rooftop. Pencil, 8 × 10. One sheet chosen.

Yours to interpret

Draw the thing that frightens you about being seen drawing. It may be a person; it may be the water.

Where it goes

The chosen sheet on the members' wall at Basic Inquiry, where members exhibit in the studio gallery. A small room, not an auditorium, on purpose.

What it shows

That the shyness can be worked like any other problem; that you will show in a room of peers.

What fails it

Drawing where nobody can see. Not hanging it.

Technique: Module 13, texture and mark-making, where anyone can see. Skill: working while seen. Portfolio: a public showing on the CV. Belonging: a members' wall among peers.

What changes in the line when someone is looking?

11 · September 2027

The large piece, and someone owns it

The constraint

Unprimed linen or exhibition panel, at least 36 × 48. Total freedom on subject. One condition, his condition: it belongs to someone else when it is finished, and you name who before you begin.

Yours to interpret

The year's thesis, whole: the body as the site of the sacred. Everything the eleven assignments taught, in one picture, at the size a wall needs.

Where it goes

Named before the first mark: Arts Umbrella's Splash auction, a collector from the list, or the November decision point. The naming is the assignment.

What it shows

That you can hold scale; that the concept is now visible; that the work has somewhere to go.

What fails it

Not naming the owner. Making it smaller.

Technique: Module 14, narrative, at scale. Skill: holding scale with a thesis. Portfolio: the piece a curator writes about. Belonging: it belongs to someone else before it is begun.

What would I make if I already knew where it was going?

12 · October 2027

A commission in care

The constraint

A body of drawings made with, not for, an organisation in care: a hospice, a hospital unit, a mental-health or disability service in Vancouver. Pencil and acrylic on paper. Sittings with people who consent, logged. Proposed in writing with a budget at CARFAC rates, as a commission or an artist-in-residence period; never entered as a contest, never free.

Yours to interpret

The subject is theirs; the seeing is yours. What the body looks like when it is being cared for, and what care looks like on a face. The drawings may be the ones the organisation keeps, the ones you keep, or both; decide it in the proposal.

Where it goes

Their walls, their annual report, their waiting room, with your name on it and a fee paid. And a public talk, in their room, with the people who sat. The field is called arts in health; Hospital Rooms in Britain is the model, and Vancouver's health foundations run art programs to be read and approached.

What it shows

That the concept works in the world, with strangers, under conditions; that you can propose, budget and deliver; that the drawing does something once it isn't for you.

What fails it

Doing it for free. Working from photographs. A single portrait where a body of work was asked for.

Technique: Module 14, narrative, and 16, experimentation, in the world. Skill: the drawn body under real conditions. Portfolio: a commission, budgeted and delivered. Belonging: the meaning of the work in a room where it is needed.

What is the drawing for, once it isn't for me?

Five terms

The nine courses run every year; what changes is their weight and their destination. Measured not by feeling but by the portfolio, the sittings, the critic's sentence and who is in the room.

Term one · 2026 to 2027
The hand back, and being seenStudio I and II at full weight. Critique from a faculty of one. Seminar: twelve texts, twelve talks. Exhibition: two juried entries that ship only if selected, one consignment submission, one public wall. Field school: the Audain. Measure: the twelve images answer the six questions a panel asks.
Term two · 2027 to 2028
One painter's week, one cohortField school becomes a week inside one painter's workshop; the room becomes the cohort. Residency: MacDowell, applied for in February 2027. Visiting artists: the first four interviews published. Technique: taught outward as the first Foundations module. Measure: a sitting from life every month, and the critic says the likeness holds.
Term three · 2028 to 2029
A body of work, and a roomStudio II becomes twenty pieces with one thesis, on panel and linen as well as paper. Exhibition: a two-person or solo show proposed in writing to the rooms on the Atlas that accept proposals. The commission in care delivered. Seminar becomes a written piece placed with a journal on the Writing page. Measure: someone who did not know me writes about the work.
Term four · 2029 to 2030
Placement, and the larger decisionConsistent placement: a consignment program, a gallery relationship that arrived organically, a public collection on paper. A Banff thematic residency if one fits. The study decision made from the portfolio and the critic's view: SFU's practice-based PhD in Contemporary Arts admits on an MA or MFA with high standing, or an established professional practice, which is what four years of sittings, books, interviews, a room and a commission are. Measure: work sells before it is finished.
Term five · 2030 to 2031
The exhibition that reads as a directionThe thesis defended in a room: the body as the site of the sacred across five years, with the sittings, the writing and the care commission inside it. Teaching from the modules. The cohort is now people I have sat with, drawn, written about and shown beside. Measure: the best I have been, by the portfolio and by the room.

What the truth of the practice is measured by

The sheets in the log, the sittings in the sitting log, the critic's sentence each month, the quarter's twelve images against the six questions, and the names of the people who have seen the work. Excelling becomes normal when those five are looked at every month and nothing else is. Don't rest on your laurels; there is only the next brief.
