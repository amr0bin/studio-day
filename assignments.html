AssignmentsOctober 2026 to October 2027

The circumstances, set once

In high school an art teacher set the circumstances I grew inside: a hard constraint on size and material, a prompt I had to interpret, and a real destination, a competition with a stage, a society that used the drawing for a year, a panel the school would own when it was finished. I asked the questions; he built the room they could be asked in. This is that structure, rebuilt for a practice restarting from inconsistency. Twelve assignments, one a month, made on the two Sundays held for larger work while the studies run underneath and twenty weekday minutes run under those, each producing one portfolio piece and one record shot. Before any of them, one assignment with no destination: the person who will look. The constraints are fixed. The interpretation never is.

Who is looking, and for what

A residency panel, a prize jury, a consignment committee, a gallerist, a curator opening a file: each looks at ten to twenty images and asks the same six things. Can this person draw from life at speed and sustain a drawing to resolution. Do they get a likeness. Can they handle a light problem and a colour problem. Is there a series, a subject worked rather than visited. Is there a large piece. And is there a work where the concept is visible without being explained. The twelve are built so that by next October the portfolio answers all six, in order, and the answer is already in a folder on the day the unknown opportunity appears.

00 · September 2026, before the rest

The seat beside the work

The constraint

One painter whose eye you trust. Not a friend who likes you; someone who draws and will say what is true. Found by the end of September. Shown the month's sheets every month, in person or by post, for the whole year.

Yours to interpret

Who. It may be someone from the Emily Carr years, a painter met at Basic Inquiry, or a stranger whose work you respect, written to plainly. The Federation runs critique programs if no one comes to mind, and that counts.

Where it goes

Nowhere. This is the one assignment with no destination, because it is the condition for the other twelve. A log entry is not a crit. Nobody was ever made ready by a mirror.

What it shows

That the work is being seen by someone who can see it.

What fails it

Reaching October without a name. Choosing someone kind instead of someone honest.

Who will tell me the truth about a drawing?

01 · October 2026

A song, in sixty gestures

The constraint

Pencil only. 8 × 10 sheets torn from the Collection paper. Two minutes each, from life or from the mirror; never from a photograph. Sixty across the month, six kept.

Yours to interpret

Choose one song. Play it on repeat for every session. The gestures are the song's shape in a body, and only you know which song it is.

Where it goes

The six kept sheets open the first Patreon post of the series and go in the file under S numbers.

What it shows

Speed; the hand ahead of the judgement; that the practice has restarted.

What fails it

Any gesture over five minutes. Any sheet from a screen. Fewer than sixty.

What does my hand still know that my eye hasn't caught up to?

02 · November 2026

One sitting, resolved

The constraint

Pencil. A half sheet, 11 × 15, torn. One person who agrees to sit, in person, for three hours in one afternoon. No retouching after the sitting ends.

Yours to interpret

Ask the sitter for a word before you begin. Draw them as that word. Do not tell anyone which word.

Where it goes

The sitter receives the record print. The sitting goes in the sitting log with date and place: this is the first drawing that qualifies for the Kingston Prize window.

What it shows

Sustained observation; likeness; that a stranger will trust you with three hours.

What fails it

Working from a photograph. Finishing it later. Not asking anyone.

Who would sit for me, and what do I owe them for it?

03 · January 2027

Self, in five values

The constraint

Acrylic on paper, 11 × 15. One lamp, curtains closed. Five values and no more; mix them before you begin and put the tubes away. The mirror, not a photograph.

Yours to interpret

A self-portrait as the answer to a question you have been avoiding. The question stays private; the answer is the painting.

Where it goes

If it holds, the Royal Society of Portrait Painters open, which closes in early February and selects international entries online. If it doesn't, the file, and the next one.

What it shows

Value control; likeness of the hardest sitter; that you will put your own face in front of a jury.

What fails it

A sixth value. Softening the light to be kind. Not entering because it 'isn't ready'.

What do I look like when nobody is arranging me?

04 · February 2027

The bright ground, shown

The constraint

Acrylic on paper, 11 × 15. The method left visible: a bright underpainting that stays at the edges, the muted layer over it, gloss to seal. Whatever is on the table at six in the evening, as it is.

Yours to interpret

The table is yours; what is on it is the ordinary. Decide what the bright colour underneath means and let it show through where it should.

Where it goes

A process post on Patreon with the three stages photographed, and the Art Rental & Sales package begun from it, because that jury wants to see a method, not a mood.

What it shows

Colour; that the method is a method; that the ordinary holds a picture.

What fails it

Covering the ground completely. Tidying the table first.

What happens to a colour when it is only allowed to show at the edges?

05 · March 2027

A table of things, one light

The constraint

Cradled panel, 12 × 16. One light source. Objects chosen for their surfaces: something matte, something glazed, something that reflects. D-rings and wire on the back before it is called finished.

Yours to interpret

A still life that is a portrait of someone who isn't in the room. Their objects, or objects that stand in for them. Nobody needs to be told who.

Where it goes

The Art Rental & Sales submission, which closes 30 April. Read the 2026 package now; make the piece the way the package asks.

What it shows

Light on form; composition; finish to a standard a consignment program will hang.

What fails it

Two light sources. A background that competes. No hardware.

Can a table hold a person?

06 · April 2027

The sitting, for a jury

The constraint

A portrait of a Canadian person you have met in person, made after a sitting, in pencil or acrylic, on paper or panel, within the Kingston Prize's size limit. The sitting logged. Made inside the window that opened 2 September 2026.

Yours to interpret

Ask the sitter to bring one object that matters to them. Whether it appears in the portrait is your decision, not theirs.

Where it goes

The Kingston Prize, entries open mid-April, deadline 1 September: one jpg, a one-page PDF, $40. The auditorium, at last, is in Toronto in November.

What it shows

Everything a portrait prize asks: likeness, life, finish, and the nerve to enter.

What fails it

A sitter you didn't meet. A portrait finished before the window opened. Not entering.

What would I want to be seen holding?

07 · May 2027

Five weeks, one subject

The constraint

Five 8 × 10s, one a Sunday for five Sundays, of the same subject in the same place at the same hour. Either medium, but the same medium all five. Released together.

Yours to interpret

Choose the subject knowing you will be tired of it by week three. What you find in weeks four and five is the point.

Where it goes

The May release, as a set of five, and one of them to the Salt Spring National Art Prize before it closes on 31 May.

What it shows

Seriality; that a subject can be worked rather than visited; discipline across a month.

What fails it

Changing the hour. Skipping a week. Choosing something easy to love.

What is left to see when the interest has worn off?

08 · June 2027

The figure in a room

The constraint

Cradled panel, 16 × 20 or larger. A person in a real room, from life, in daylight from the window. At least three sessions.

Yours to interpret

A room where the body is safe. It may be your room. Decide what makes it safe and paint that, not the furniture.

Where it goes

The centre spread of the annual book, and the piece the November decision point is judged on.

What it shows

Scale; the figure in space; that the concept can be painted without being explained.

What fails it

A room from a photograph. Explaining it in the title.

Where does the body stop being watched?

09 · July 2027

A master study

The constraint

Pencil, 8 × 10, from a reproduction, since Foundations is being recorded this month and the Sundays are halved. One painting from the Atlas: one of the Carrs at the Audain, a Hammershøi, a Morandi, the Fabritius.

Yours to interpret

Copy it to find its decisions, not its surface. Write the three decisions you found on the back.

Where it goes

A Patreon post: the study, and the three decisions. The habit of reading a painting this way is the real product.

What it shows

That you can read a picture; that a small study can hold a large idea.

What fails it

Copying the surface. More than an hour and a half.

What did they decide before they picked up the brush?

10 · August 2027

In public

The constraint

Three sessions drawn where people can watch: a Basic Inquiry session, the seawall at English Bay, the Public Library's rooftop. Pencil, 8 × 10. One sheet chosen.

Yours to interpret

Draw the thing that frightens you about being seen drawing. It may be a person; it may be the water.

Where it goes

The chosen sheet on the members' wall at Basic Inquiry, where members exhibit in the studio gallery. A small room, not an auditorium, on purpose.

What it shows

That the shyness can be worked like any other problem; that you will show in a room of peers.

What fails it

Drawing where nobody can see. Not hanging it.

What changes in the line when someone is looking?

11 · September 2027

The large piece, and someone owns it

The constraint

Unprimed linen or exhibition panel, at least 36 × 48. Total freedom on subject. One condition, his condition: it belongs to someone else when it is finished, and you name who before you begin.

Yours to interpret

The year's thesis, whole: the body as the site of the sacred. Everything the eleven assignments taught, in one picture, at the size a wall needs.

Where it goes

Named before the first mark: Arts Umbrella's Splash auction, a collector from the list, or the November decision point. The naming is the assignment.

What it shows

That you can hold scale; that the concept is now visible; that the work has somewhere to go.

What fails it

Not naming the owner. Making it smaller.

What would I make if I already knew where it was going?

12 · October 2027

The competition, for a cause

The constraint

Pencil, at poster proportion, for an organisation that will use it: a mental-health, disability or care organisation in Vancouver with a real need for an image. The image must work at their size, in their print.

Yours to interpret

The drawing for a cause, again, years on, with what you know now about the body and its limits. The subject is theirs; the interpretation is yours.

Where it goes

The organisation's use of it for a year, and whatever stage comes with that. To be found: the pattern is a cause with a real need, not a contest with a prize.

What it shows

The whole year, in one drawing that leaves the studio and does work in the world.

What fails it

Making it for the file. Refusing the stage.

What is the drawing for, once it isn't for me?

The reviews

At the end of January, April, July and October the twelve-image portfolio is rebuilt from what exists, against the six questions above, and the readiness kit's images are replaced. A piece that fails its brief is not thrown out; it goes in the file and the assignment is repeated in a later month if a Sunday allows. The November 2027 decision point on the Year reads this portfolio, not the plan.

How the pieces are kept

Portfolio pieces are numbered P001 onward in the catalogue, apart from the studies' S numbers, so the two bodies of work stay distinct. Each one is recorded on the Monday like everything else, and the sitting log carries the sittings that the portrait assignments depend on.

What is his in this

The three parts, and the order of them. The constraint before the freedom, so that the freedom has something to press against. The destination named before the work begins, so that the work is made for somewhere. And the stage: at least three of the twelve end with someone else's hands on the piece and your name said aloud in a room. That was the part that terrified you then and the part that did the most. It still will. He also said not to rest on your laurels, and this page is built so there is nothing to rest on: only the next brief.
