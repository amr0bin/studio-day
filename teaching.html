Foundation year, reconsidered

Everything covered in Foundation year at Emily Carr, taken up again after fifteen years of professional work in learning design. The question it answers is the one I keep coming back to: what would I want to learn now, if I could do that year again as an adult with experience?

What it is for

Most online painting courses are built for painters who already know what they are missing: colour, composition, the head. Foundations is for people without a framework at all. Adults returning to art, painters who taught themselves, anyone who did a degree twenty years ago and wants to rebuild from the ground.

My claim on the material is specific. I went through that curriculum, then spent fifteen years studying how people actually learn, in health, higher education, research and the public sector. The second half is the part almost no artist teaching online can offer.

There is a further layer I have not decided about. Foundation year is often taught in ways that damage people: comparison, critique culture, the body treated as a problem to correct. My thesis is about exactly that. A version of Foundations could reconsider the conditions under which the material becomes learnable, and not only the material.

The format

Six weeks, three hours a week, in the same rhythm Foundation year ran in. Eighteen hours to a module, which is roughly double what most artist-led online courses offer.

Recorded, not live. Students work at their own pace and the module stays on sale afterwards. A live cohort would mean a fixed weekly commitment for six consecutive weeks, and once graduate study starts that commitment is the first thing that would break.

A flipped version is possible much further out: the recordings as preparation, then a monthly critique group online or at my table. That is a decision for a year when the recorded modules already exist and the schedule has proved itself. It is not on the table now.

One module a year

Foundations is the series. Each annual course is one module of it, and the divisions follow whatever the material actually wants: drawing, colour, composition, the body, materials.

Each module stands alone and sells alone. Someone who takes the first has a reason to come back for the next four, and after five years there is a body of work rather than five unrelated courses.

The arithmetic, honestly

Recording changes the numbers, and not in my favour. Eighteen hours of finished video is a large edit. At the rate a monthly process video takes, this is somewhere between forty and sixty hours of editing, and Monday is the only block that holds it.

Three ways through, none of them free:

  • Record and edit across the whole spring, using the Monday video block, and skip the monthly process video for those months.
  • Cut the module to four sessions of two hours. Twelve hours of finished video against Zoey Frank's ten, still substantial, and roughly half the edit.
  • Record with minimal editing. Fixed camera, no cuts, no titles beyond a card at the top. Closer to a filmed lecture than a produced course, and much cheaper to make.

The third option is the one I would test first. It costs the least and it can be improved in later years once a module has proved there is an audience.

Pricing and the first cohort

Zoey Frank's pre-recorded courses run from $185 to $350 for four sessions of about two and a half hours. Eighteen hours supports the upper end of that, or above it. A first module priced low, sold to ten or twelve people mostly from Emily Carr, buys me the recording, the testimonials and real evidence of what works before I charge full price.

It is also a much easier thing to ask of that network than anything else on my list.

Still open

  • Which module comes first.
  • Six three-hour sessions, four two-hour sessions, or lightly edited recordings at full length.
  • Whether the pedagogical layer is named in the course itself or simply built into it.
  • Where it is hosted and how the video is delivered.
  • The public name. Foundations is hard to search for and hard to protect, and may work better as the internal architecture.
  • How the course language refers to Emily Carr without implying affiliation or endorsement.
